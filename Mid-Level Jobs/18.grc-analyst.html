90-Day Roadmap For "GRC Analyst" Mastery

An intermediate-track plan for learners who understand basic IT/security concepts and are ready to specialize in governance, risk management, and compliance — the frameworks, audits, and risk language that keep organizations secure and accountable.

Phase 1: GRC Fundamentals & Core Frameworks

Days 1-30: Governance structures, risk language, and the major frameworks every GRC Analyst must know

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

NIST CSF / RMF ISO 27001 Risk Assessment Policy Writing Compliance Mapping

Week 1: GRC Foundations & Vocabulary

  • Learn the GRC triad: what Governance, Risk, and Compliance each actually mean in practice
  • Study the three lines of defense model (operations, risk/compliance, audit)
  • Learn core risk vocabulary: threat, vulnerability, likelihood, impact, inherent vs. residual risk
  • Study the difference between a policy, standard, procedure, and guideline
  • Read the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF 2.0) Core functions end-to-end once
  • Map out how GRC interacts with IT, security, legal, and the board
  • Learn what a control is, and the difference between preventive, detective, and corrective controls
  • Document 20 GRC terms in your own glossary with plain-language definitions
NIST CSF 2.0 Risk Terminology Three Lines of Defense Controls

Task heading at the end of week: GRC Glossary & Org Map

Build a personal GRC glossary (20+ terms) and a one-page diagram showing how governance, risk, and compliance functions interact in a typical mid-size company.

Week 2: ISO 27001 & ISMS Basics

  • Study the ISO 27001 structure: clauses 4-10 and Annex A controls
  • Learn what an ISMS (Information Security Management System) actually requires
  • Study the Statement of Applicability (SoA) and why it's central to ISO audits
  • Learn the PDCA (Plan-Do-Check-Act) cycle as it applies to continuous improvement
  • Compare ISO 27001 against NIST CSF and SOC 2 at a high level — overlaps and differences
  • Study how internal audits feed into ISMS management review meetings
  • Review a sample ISO 27001 Annex A control list and group controls by theme
  • Practice writing one short policy statement (e.g. Access Control Policy) following ISO structure
ISO 27001 ISMS Annex A Controls SoA

Task heading at the end of week: Mini Statement of Applicability

Build a simplified SoA for a fictional small company, marking 15 Annex A controls as applicable/not applicable with justification for each.

Week 3: Risk Assessment & Risk Registers

  • Learn qualitative vs. quantitative risk assessment methods
  • Study risk matrices: likelihood x impact scoring and heat maps
  • Learn FAIR (Factor Analysis of Information Risk) basics as a quantitative model
  • Practice building a risk register: asset, threat, vulnerability, existing controls, residual risk
  • Study risk treatment options: accept, avoid, transfer, mitigate
  • Learn how risk appetite and risk tolerance differ, and why leadership sets them
  • Practice scoring 10 realistic organizational risks using a 5x5 risk matrix
  • Study how risk registers feed into audit planning and resource prioritization
Risk Register FAIR Model Risk Matrix Risk Treatment

Task heading at the end of week: Sample Risk Register

Build a 10-row risk register in a spreadsheet for a fictional org, with scored likelihood/impact, treatment decision, and a one-line justification per risk.

Week 4: Compliance Landscape & Regulatory Mapping

  • Study major regulations at a working level: GDPR, HIPAA, PCI-DSS, SOX
  • Learn how SOC 2 Type I vs Type II audits differ and what each proves
  • Study control mapping: how one control (e.g. MFA) can satisfy multiple frameworks at once
  • Learn the basics of data privacy principles: lawful basis, data minimization, retention
  • Study how PCI-DSS scoping works for organizations handling card data
  • Learn what a compliance calendar/tracker looks like in practice
  • Practice cross-mapping 5 controls across NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and SOC 2
  • Review one real (public) SOC 2 report summary to see report structure in practice
GDPR/HIPAA/PCI-DSS SOC 2 Control Mapping Compliance Calendar

Task heading at the end of week: Cross-Framework Control Map

Build a control mapping spreadsheet showing how 8 common controls satisfy requirements across NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and SOC 2 simultaneously.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You understand core GRC vocabulary, can navigate ISO 27001 and NIST CSF structures, and can build a working risk register and cross-framework control map — the foundational toolkit of a GRC Analyst.

Hint: One concept per piece (risk register, ISMS, SOC 2, etc.), beginner-friendly language.

Phase 2: Audits, Controls Testing & Vendor Risk

Days 31-60: Move from theory into the practical mechanics of audits, control testing, and third-party risk

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Internal Audit Controls Testing Vendor Risk (TPRM) GRC Platforms Business Continuity

Week 5: Internal Audit Fundamentals

  • Learn the internal audit lifecycle: planning, fieldwork, reporting, follow-up
  • Study audit evidence types: documentation review, interviews, observation, re-performance
  • Learn how to write an audit finding: condition, criteria, cause, effect, recommendation
  • Study sampling methodology basics for control testing (when full population testing isn't feasible)
  • Learn the difference between a design test and an operating effectiveness test
  • Practice writing audit interview questions for a access-control review
  • Study how audit findings get rated (e.g. high/medium/low severity) and tracked to closure
  • Review a sample internal audit report structure end-to-end
Audit Lifecycle Audit Evidence Finding Writing Sampling

Task heading at the end of week: Mock Audit Finding Set

Write 5 mock audit findings (condition/criteria/cause/effect/recommendation format) based on a fictional access-control review scenario.

Week 6: Controls Testing in Practice

  • Learn how to design a control testing plan for a specific Annex A or NIST control
  • Practice testing access reviews: pulling a user list, sampling accounts, checking approvals
  • Study change management control testing (how to verify proper approval/testing/rollback)
  • Learn how to test logging and monitoring controls (existence vs. operating effectiveness)
  • Study segregation of duties (SoD) concepts and how to test for conflicts
  • Practice documenting a control test workpaper with evidence references
  • Learn common control gaps auditors flag repeatedly (stale access, missing approvals, weak logging)
  • Build a personal control-testing checklist template you can reuse
Access Reviews Change Management SoD Workpapers

Task heading at the end of week: Control Test Workpaper

Build a complete workpaper testing a fictional company's quarterly user access review, including sample size rationale, evidence checklist, and conclusion.

Week 7: Third-Party / Vendor Risk Management (TPRM)

  • Study why vendor risk matters: supply chain breaches and fourth-party risk concepts
  • Learn vendor risk tiering: how criticality and data access determine assessment depth
  • Practice reviewing a vendor security questionnaire (SIG Lite or CAIQ style)
  • Study how to evaluate a vendor's SOC 2 report as part of due diligence
  • Learn what belongs in a vendor risk assessment report and remediation tracking
  • Study contract security clauses: right to audit, breach notification, data handling terms
  • Practice scoring 5 fictional vendors by risk tier based on data sensitivity and access level
  • Learn how ongoing vendor monitoring differs from one-time onboarding assessment
TPRM SIG/CAIQ SOC 2 Review Vendor Tiering

Task heading at the end of week: Vendor Risk Assessment

Complete a full vendor risk assessment for a fictional SaaS vendor: questionnaire review, risk tier assignment, and a remediation tracker for 3 gaps found.

Week 8: GRC Platforms & Business Continuity

  • Explore a GRC platform hands-on (e.g. free trial/demo of Vanta, Drata, OneTrust, or ServiceNow GRC)
  • Learn how automated evidence collection works in modern compliance platforms
  • Study how a GRC platform maps controls to multiple frameworks automatically
  • Learn Business Continuity Planning (BCP) and Disaster Recovery (DR) fundamentals
  • Study RTO (Recovery Time Objective) and RPO (Recovery Point Objective) concepts
  • Practice building a simple Business Impact Analysis (BIA) for a fictional critical system
  • Learn how incident response plans connect to GRC reporting obligations (breach notification timelines)
  • Document how you'd structure a compliance dashboard for leadership reporting
GRC Platforms BCP/DR BIA RTO/RPO

Task heading at the end of week: Business Impact Analysis

Write a BIA for one critical fictional business system, defining RTO/RPO and the downstream impact of an outage on operations and compliance.

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You can plan and document control tests, run a vendor risk assessment, navigate a GRC platform, and build a business impact analysis — the practical mechanics that fill a GRC Analyst's day-to-day work.

Phase 3: Reporting, Stakeholder Communication & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Executive reporting, audit-readiness, certification prep, and the job-search push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Executive Reporting Audit Readiness Policy Lifecycle Cert Prep Interview Readiness

Week 9: Executive Reporting & Risk Communication

  • Study how to translate technical risk into business language for executives and the board
  • Learn how to build a risk heat map / dashboard summarizing top organizational risks
  • Practice writing a one-page executive risk summary from a detailed risk register
  • Study KPI vs. KRI (Key Risk Indicator) — what leadership actually wants tracked
  • Learn how to present "risk acceptance" decisions for sign-off in plain, defensible language
  • Practice building a simple compliance status dashboard (red/yellow/green by framework)
  • Study how board-level risk committees typically structure their quarterly reviews
  • Record yourself presenting one risk finding in under 2 minutes, executive-style
Risk Heat Maps KRI/KPI Executive Summaries Board Reporting

Task heading at the end of week: Executive Risk Dashboard

Build a one-page executive dashboard summarizing your Phase 1 risk register: top 5 risks, heat map, and recommended actions in board-ready language.

Week 10: Audit Readiness & Evidence Management

  • Learn how to prepare an organization for an external audit (ISO certification or SOC 2)
  • Study evidence management best practices: naming, versioning, and central evidence repositories
  • Practice building an audit evidence request tracker mapped to specific controls
  • Learn how to run a pre-audit gap assessment and prioritize remediation by risk and effort
  • Study common reasons audits fail or get delayed (missing evidence, stale policies, no owner)
  • Practice writing a remediation plan with owners, deadlines, and status tracking
  • Learn how corrective action plans (CAPs) work after a failed control test
  • Build a personal "audit-ready" checklist template you can reuse on future engagements
Evidence Management Gap Assessment CAPs Audit-Readiness

Task heading at the end of week: Pre-Audit Gap Assessment

Run a mock gap assessment against 15 ISO 27001 Annex A controls for a fictional company, producing a prioritized remediation plan with owners and deadlines.

Week 11: Policy Lifecycle Management & Awareness

  • Learn the full policy lifecycle: drafting, review, approval, publishing, periodic review, retirement
  • Study how policy exceptions should be requested, approved, and tracked
  • Practice writing a policy review/update cycle calendar for a fictional 12-policy set
  • Learn how security awareness training programs tie back to compliance requirements
  • Study how to measure training effectiveness (completion rates, phishing test results)
  • Practice drafting a short awareness communication explaining a new policy to employees
  • Learn how policy violations are typically escalated and documented
  • Build your own reusable policy template with version control fields built in
Policy Lifecycle Exception Tracking Awareness Training Version Control

Task heading at the end of week: Policy Lifecycle Pack

Build a reusable policy template with version history fields, plus a 12-policy annual review calendar and one sample employee awareness email.

Week 12: Certification & Interview Readiness

  • Review practice questions aligned to a target cert (e.g. CompTIA Security+, ISO 27001 Lead Implementer/Auditor, or CRISC)
  • Do a full mock audit walkthrough under time pressure using one of your earlier risk registers
  • Prepare answers for common GRC interview questions (explain a risk register, walk through an audit finding, etc.)
  • Build a one-page resume bullet list translating your 90 days into measurable skills/achievements
  • Compile your strongest artifacts (risk register, gap assessment, executive dashboard) into one portfolio
  • Practice a mock technical interview (explain ISO 27001 vs SOC 2 to a non-technical hiring manager)
  • Research target companies/industries (finance, healthcare, SaaS) and tailor your resume accordingly
  • Plan your next 90 days: pick a specialization (privacy/GDPR, audit, or vendor risk lead)
Mock Interviews Resume Building Portfolio Building Cert Prep

Task heading at the end of week: Job-Ready GRC Portfolio

Finalize a portfolio folder (risk register, gap assessment, executive dashboard, policy pack), resume bullets, and a 2-minute mock interview recording.

🏆 Final Achievement

Congratulations! You now have a working GRC toolkit (frameworks, risk registers, control testing, vendor risk, audit readiness, executive reporting) plus a portfolio of real artifacts and a job-ready application package — the foundation of a genuinely employable GRC Analyst.

What Next

Where to take your GRC career after Day 90

1. Specialize

  • Pick one lane: audit, risk management, vendor risk, or privacy
  • Go deep on one regulatory area (GDPR/HIPAA/PCI) as a specialization
  • Consider a privacy-focused track (CIPP/E, CIPM) as a growth area
  • Explore IT audit (CISA) as a longer-term specialization
  • Look into GRC platform administration as a technical-adjacent niche
  • Consider security architecture/governance as a senior path
  • Specialize in one industry vertical (healthcare, finance, SaaS)
  • Pursue an advanced cert aligned to your chosen lane
  • Join a specialized community for that niche
  • Set a 6-month deep-specialization goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to entry-level GRC Analyst / Compliance Analyst roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description using your portfolio artifacts
  • Attend local ISACA/IIA meetups and introduce yourself
  • Reach out to GRC professionals on LinkedIn for informational chats
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio folder updated with new artifacts
  • Practice mock interviews monthly to stay sharp
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider contract/temp GRC support gigs to build experience
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly regulatory/framework update news
  • Re-test old risk registers and assessments with new knowledge quarterly
  • Write a monthly post about something you learned in GRC
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio project every 90 days
  • Track major framework updates (e.g. NIST CSF, ISO 27001 revisions)
  • Mentor beginners — teaching reinforces your own mastery
  • Read one new GRC/risk book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference or webinar per month
  • Get hands-on with a new GRC platform trial periodically
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create a beginner-friendly GRC series for your audience
  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (risk register, policy pack) for others
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on one GRC topic
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly
  • Pair up with another learner for accountability
  • Contribute feedback or templates to a community resource hub
  • Review and give feedback on a peer's risk register or audit finding
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone starting out

Remember: GRC is the discipline of turning "we should be secure" into "here's exactly how we prove it, track it, and improve it." Every framework, audit, and risk register exists to answer one question: "can we demonstrate control over what could go wrong?" Stay precise, document everything, and keep asking why a control exists before you test it.
