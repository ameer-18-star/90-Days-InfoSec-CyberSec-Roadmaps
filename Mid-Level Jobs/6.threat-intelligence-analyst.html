90-Day Roadmap For "Threat Intelligence Analyst"

This 90-day roadmap is designed to equip you with the foundational knowledge and practical skills necessary to excel as a Threat Intelligence Analyst. You'll move from understanding the basics of cybersecurity to advanced intelligence gathering and analysis.

Phase 1: Foundation

Days 1-30: Building Your Core Cybersecurity & Threat Intelligence Understanding

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Cybersecurity Fundamentals Networking Basics Operating Systems Threat Landscape & Actors Introduction to Threat Intelligence

Week 1: Cybersecurity & Networking Fundamentals

  • Understand core cybersecurity concepts (CIA triad, risk, vulnerability, threat).
  • Learn basic networking protocols (TCP/IP, HTTP/S, DNS).
  • Familiarize yourself with common network devices (routers, switches, firewalls).
  • Explore network topologies and segmentation.
  • Grasp the fundamentals of operating systems (Windows, Linux command line basics).
CompTIA Security+ Network+ Linux Basics

Task: Network Map & Vulnerability Identification

Create a simple network diagram of a small office or home network and identify potential vulnerabilities within it.

Week 2: Introduction to Threat Landscape & Actors

  • Understand common cyberattack types (malware, phishing, DoS, ransomware).
  • Research different types of threat actors (nation-state, cybercrime, hacktivists).
  • Learn about the motivations and TTPs (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures) of various threat groups.
  • Explore historical significant cyber incidents and their impact.
  • Understand the concept of the kill chain and MITRE ATT&CK framework.
MITRE ATT&CK Cyber Kill Chain OSINT Basics

Task: Threat Actor Profile Summary

Choose one prominent threat actor group and compile a brief report on their history, common targets, and known TTPs using publicly available information.

Week 3: Foundational Threat Intelligence Concepts

  • Define Threat Intelligence and its various types (strategic, operational, tactical).
  • Understand the intelligence lifecycle (Direction, Collection, Processing, Analysis, Dissemination, Feedback).
  • Learn about the importance of context and actionable intelligence.
  • Explore different sources of threat intelligence (OSINT, commercial feeds, dark web).
  • Begin to understand indicators of compromise (IOCs) and their role.
Threat Intelligence Platforms (TIPs - conceptual) STIX/TAXII (conceptual)

Task: Identify Threat Intelligence Needs

Imagine you're securing a small e-commerce business. Identify 3-5 types of threat intelligence that would be most valuable to them and explain why.

Week 4: Data Analysis & Visualization Basics

  • Introduction to data analysis techniques for security data.
  • Learn basic scripting for data manipulation (Python fundamentals).
  • Understand how to extract relevant information from logs and network data.
  • Familiarize yourself with basic data visualization principles.
  • Practice identifying patterns and anomalies in datasets.
Python (basics) Excel/Google Sheets Regex (basics)

Task: Simple Log Analysis Script

Write a basic Python script that can parse a simplified log file (provided or self-created) and extract IP addresses or specific error messages.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You've built a solid foundation in cybersecurity and the core concepts of threat intelligence. You can identify fundamental threats, understand basic networking, and appreciate the value of intelligence.

Projects After Phase 1

These projects will help solidify your understanding of Phase 1 concepts. Choose one to work on.

Threat Actor Research Report

Conduct in-depth research on a recent, publicly reported cyberattack. Detail the threat actor, their TTPs, the target, and the impact.

Hints: Use public news articles, cybersecurity blogs, and threat intelligence reports from reputable sources.

Basic OSINT Investigation

Using OSINT techniques, gather information about a fictional company or a publicly available (with permission) target. Focus on identifying potential attack vectors or exposed information.

Hints: Use tools like Google Dorks, Shodan, Whois, social media searches.

Network Defense Strategy Outline

Given a hypothetical small business, outline a basic network defense strategy incorporating firewalls, IDS/IPS (conceptual), and endpoint protection, explaining how each protects against common threats.

Hints: Consider common attack surfaces and how different security layers address them.

Automated IOC Extraction Script

Develop a Python script that can read a text file (simulating a threat intelligence report) and automatically extract all IPv4 addresses and URLs.

Hints: Use regular expressions (regex) to match IP address and URL patterns.

Phase 2: Intermediate Concepts

Days 31-60: Deep Dive into Threat Intelligence Frameworks, Tools, and Analysis

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Threat Intelligence Frameworks IOC Management Basic Malware Analysis Vulnerability Management Report Writing

Week 5: Threat Intelligence Frameworks & Standards

  • Gain a deeper understanding of the MITRE ATT&CK framework and its practical application.
  • Learn about STIX (Structured Threat Information Expression) and TAXII (Trusted Automated eXchange of Indicator Information) for sharing threat intelligence.
  • Explore other intelligence models like Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis.
  • Understand the concept of threat intelligence platforms (TIPs) and their features.
  • Practice mapping observed TTPs to ATT&CK techniques.
MITRE ATT&CK STIX/TAXII MISP (conceptual)

Task: ATT&CK Mapping Exercise

Given a simplified incident report, identify specific attacker actions and map them to relevant MITRE ATT&CK techniques.

Week 6: Indicator of Compromise (IOC) Management & Feeds

  • Learn about different types of IOCs (IP addresses, domains, file hashes, URLs).
  • Understand how IOCs are collected, enriched, and shared.
  • Explore various public and commercial threat intelligence feeds.
  • Practice integrating and utilizing IOCs in a simulated SIEM or security tool.
  • Learn about the challenges of IOC management (false positives, aging IOCs).
VirusTotal Shodan Open-source TI Feeds

Task: IOC Enrichment & Analysis

Take a list of 10-15 suspicious IOCs (e.g., from an open-source feed) and use online tools (VirusTotal, AlienVault OTX) to gather more information and determine their maliciousness.

Week 7: Basic Malware Analysis & Reverse Engineering Concepts

  • Understand different malware types (viruses, worms, trojans, rootkits).
  • Introduction to static malware analysis (string extraction, header analysis).
  • Introduction to dynamic malware analysis (sandboxing, process monitoring).
  • Learn about basic reverse engineering concepts (assembly language basics, disassemblers - conceptual).
  • Understand how malware uses IOCs and TTPs.
Cuckoo Sandbox (conceptual) Ghidra/IDA Pro (conceptual) PEStudio

Task: Malware Report Summary

Analyze a sample malware report (publicly available) and summarize the key findings, including its functionality, propagation method, and any identified IOCs.

Week 8: Vulnerability Management & Threat Modeling

  • Understand the lifecycle of vulnerabilities (discovery, exploitation, patching).
  • Learn about common vulnerability scoring systems (CVSS).
  • Explore how threat intelligence informs vulnerability management priorities.
  • Introduction to basic threat modeling methodologies (e.g., STRIDE).
  • Practice identifying and prioritizing vulnerabilities based on threat intelligence.
NVD (National Vulnerability Database) CVSS OWASP Top 10

Task: Vulnerability Prioritization Scenario

Given a list of vulnerabilities and a specific organizational context, prioritize them based on CVSS scores and perceived threat intelligence relevance.

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You've gained intermediate knowledge of threat intelligence frameworks, can manage IOCs, understand basic malware analysis, and apply threat intelligence to vulnerability management. You're ready to start producing actionable intelligence!

Projects After Phase 2

Apply your growing expertise with these intermediate projects. Choose one to work on.

Threat Intelligence Report (Operational)

Write an operational threat intelligence report on a current, trending cyber threat (e.g., a specific ransomware variant or phishing campaign). Include IOCs, TTPs, and recommendations for defense.

Hints: Use open-source threat intelligence blogs, security vendor reports, and news.

Automated IOC De-duplication and Normalization Script

Develop a Python script that takes a list of IOCs (IPs, URLs, hashes) with potential duplicates and inconsistent formatting, then cleans and de-duplicates them.

Hints: Use Python sets for de-duplication and string manipulation for normalization.

Simulated Sandbox Analysis Report

If possible, use a free online sandbox (e.g., Any.Run free tier) to analyze a benign file or a known malware sample (with extreme caution in a safe environment). Document your observations and extracted IOCs.

Hints: Focus on process tree, network connections, and file system changes.

Threat Modeling Exercise (Small Application)

Perform a basic threat modeling exercise for a simple web application (e.g., a login page). Identify potential threats using a framework like STRIDE and suggest countermeasures informed by threat intelligence.

Hints: Consider common web vulnerabilities (OWASP Top 10) and how they could be exploited by different threat actors.

Phase 3: Production & Dissemination

Days 61-90: Generating, Communicating, and Integrating Threat Intelligence

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Intelligence Dissemination Structured Analysis Advanced OSINT Threat Hunting Integration Stakeholder Communication

Week 9: Intelligence Dissemination & Reporting

  • Master the art of writing clear, concise, and actionable threat intelligence reports.
  • Understand tailoring intelligence for different audiences (executives, security analysts, incident responders).
  • Learn about different dissemination channels and formats.
  • Practice presenting threat intelligence findings verbally.
  • Focus on the "So what?" and "Now what?" aspects of intelligence.
Technical Writing Presentation Skills

Task: Executive Summary Creation

Convert a detailed technical threat intelligence report into a concise, high-level executive summary suitable for non-technical leadership.

Week 10: Structured Analytic Techniques & Pivoting

  • Explore Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs) like Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) and Brainstorming.
  • Learn advanced pivoting techniques to uncover related IOCs, TTPs, and threat actors.
  • Practice using graph databases or relationship mapping for complex threat analysis.
  • Understand the importance of cognitive biases in intelligence analysis and how to mitigate them.
Maltego (conceptual) Palantir Foundry (conceptual) Graph Databases (conceptual)

Task: ACH Scenario Application

Apply the Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) technique to a simulated threat scenario with ambiguous evidence to arrive at a most probable conclusion.

Week 11: Advanced OSINT & Dark Web Monitoring

  • Deepen your OSINT skills with advanced search operators and specialized tools.
  • Understand the ethical considerations and legal aspects of OSINT.
  • Introduction to dark web monitoring concepts and tools (e.g., Tor, I2P – for understanding, not active participation without proper authorization).
  • Learn how to safely and legally gather intelligence from less accessible sources.
  • Focus on attribution and identifying potential sources of highly sensitive information.
Advanced Google Dorking Dark Web Monitoring Tools (conceptual) Social Media Intelligence

Task: Deep Dive OSINT Investigation

Conduct a deep-dive OSINT investigation into a fictional company or a public breach, aiming to uncover highly specific details about the attack or vulnerable systems.

Week 12: Threat Intelligence Program Development & Integration

  • Understand how threat intelligence integrates into various security operations (SOC, IR, VM).
  • Learn about building and maturing a threat intelligence program within an organization.
  • Explore concepts like automation of intelligence feeds and security orchestration, automation, and response (SOAR).
  • Capstone project: Synthesize all learned skills into a comprehensive threat intelligence exercise.
  • Prepare for real-world scenarios and continuous learning.
SIEM Integration SOAR (conceptual) Playbooks

Task: Threat Intelligence Program Proposal

Develop a high-level proposal for establishing or improving a threat intelligence program for a medium-sized enterprise, outlining key components, goals, and integration points.

🏆 Final Achievement

Congratulations! You've completed a rigorous 90-day roadmap. You are now equipped with the advanced knowledge and practical experience to function effectively as a Threat Intelligence Analyst, capable of producing, disseminating, and integrating high-quality intelligence.

Projects After Phase 3 (Advanced/Capstone)

These capstone projects will challenge you to synthesize all your learned skills and are highly recommended for portfolio building.

End-to-End Threat Intelligence Cycle Simulation

Simulate a complete threat intelligence cycle: from identifying a new threat, collecting relevant data, analyzing it, producing a report for different stakeholders, and outlining how it integrates into security operations.

Hints: Use a real-world, recent threat as your case study. Detail each step of the intelligence lifecycle.

Custom Threat Intelligence Feed Development

Create a simple Python script to collect IOCs from multiple open-source feeds, de-duplicate them, enrich them using public APIs (e.g., VirusTotal), and format them into a basic STIX/TAXII compatible format.

Hints: Look into Python libraries for API calls and JSON manipulation. Explore existing STIX libraries.

Adversary Emulation Plan based on TI

Develop an adversary emulation plan for a red team exercise, specifically leveraging threat intelligence on a known threat actor. Detail the TTPs to emulate and the expected indicators.

Hints: Focus on a specific threat actor from the MITRE ATT&CK framework and build scenarios around their common behaviors.

Building a Personal Threat Intelligence Dashboard

Use open-source tools (e.g., ELK Stack, Splunk Free) or basic web development skills to create a simple dashboard that visualizes threat intelligence data you've collected or generated.

Hints: Focus on key metrics like top IOCs, active threats, and TTP trends.

What Next

Your journey doesn't end after 90 days. Here's how to keep growing.

1. Specialize and Deepen Expertise

  • **Malware Analysis:** Focus on advanced static and dynamic analysis, reverse engineering.
  • **Vulnerability Research:** Dive into finding and exploiting vulnerabilities.
  • **Geopolitical/Strategic TI:** Develop expertise in specific regions or state-sponsored threats.
  • **Network Forensics:** Master analysis of network traffic for threat detection.
  • **Cloud Threat Intelligence:** Focus on threats specific to cloud environments (AWS, Azure, GCP).

2. Contribute to the Community

  • **Blog about your findings:** Share your analysis of new threats or techniques.
  • **Contribute to open-source TI projects:** Get involved with MISP, YARA rules, or other community efforts.
  • **Present at local meetups:** Share your knowledge and network with peers.
  • **Write CTF (Capture The Flag) challenges:** Helps solidify your understanding by designing problems.

3. Continuous Learning & Networking

  • **Stay Current:** Follow leading cybersecurity news outlets, threat intelligence blogs, and research papers.
  • **Advanced Certifications:** Consider certifications like GIAC GCTI or more specialized ones.
  • **Network:** Connect with other threat intelligence professionals on LinkedIn and at conferences.
  • **Practice Regularly:** Keep your skills sharp with CTFs, home lab exercises, and active analysis.

4. Explore Adjacent Fields

  • **Incident Response:** Threat intelligence is critical for effective IR.
  • **Security Operations Center (SOC) Analyst:** Apply TI directly in a defensive role.
  • **Red Teaming/Penetration Testing:** Understanding how attackers operate enhances TI.
  • **Digital Forensics:** Investigating breaches to gather intelligence.
  • **Data Science/Machine Learning for Security:** Apply advanced analytical methods to large security datasets.
