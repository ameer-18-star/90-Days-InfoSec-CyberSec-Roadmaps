90-Day Roadmap For "IAM Analyst" Mastery

An intermediate-track plan for learners who understand basic IT/security concepts and are ready to specialize in identity lifecycle management, access governance, and the directory/authentication systems that decide who can reach what.

Phase 1: Identity Fundamentals & Directory Services

Days 1-30: Identity lifecycle concepts, authentication/authorization mechanics, and directory service fundamentals

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Identity Lifecycle Active Directory Authentication Protocols RBAC/ABAC MFA

Week 1: Identity & Access Fundamentals

  • Study the core IAM triad: identification, authentication, and authorization — and how they differ
  • Learn the identity lifecycle stages: joiner, mover, leaver (JML) and why each transition is a risk point
  • Study identity types: human users, service accounts, machine identities, and privileged accounts
  • Learn the difference between authentication factors: something you know/have/are, and factor strength
  • Study how identity underpins zero trust — "never trust, always verify" in practical terms
  • Read one real-world IAM-related breach case study (e.g. an orphaned account or excessive access incident)
  • Learn the basics of single sign-on (SSO) conceptually before touching any specific protocol
  • Document 20 IAM terms in your own glossary (JML, SoD, RBAC, federation, provisioning, etc.)
AuthN vs AuthZ JML Lifecycle Zero Trust SSO Basics

Task heading at the end of week: IAM Glossary & Breach Case Study

Build a personal 20-term IAM glossary and write a one-page breakdown of a real orphaned-account or excessive-access breach, mapping it to a JML lifecycle failure.

Week 2: Active Directory & Directory Services

  • Study Active Directory architecture: domains, forests, organizational units (OUs), trees
  • Set up a small lab AD environment (Windows Server + domain-joined client) for hands-on practice
  • Learn Group Policy Objects (GPOs) and how they enforce baseline security settings
  • Study AD groups: security groups vs. distribution groups, and nested group risks
  • Learn how LDAP works conceptually as the protocol underlying directory queries
  • Study Azure AD/Entra ID as the cloud-native directory evolution of on-prem AD
  • Practice creating users, groups, and OUs in your lab AD, then applying a basic GPO
  • Learn common AD attack paths at a defensive-awareness level (Kerberoasting, excessive group nesting)
Active Directory GPOs LDAP Azure AD/Entra ID

Task heading at the end of week: Lab AD Build

Build a small AD lab with 3 OUs, 5 users, 2 security groups, and one GPO enforcing a password policy; document your structure with a diagram.

Week 3: Authentication Protocols & Federation

  • Study SAML 2.0 flow: identity provider, service provider, assertions, and where trust is established
  • Learn OAuth 2.0 and OpenID Connect (OIDC) — and how they differ from SAML and from each other
  • Study Kerberos basics: tickets, the KDC, and how it underpins AD authentication
  • Learn how SSO actually reduces risk (fewer credentials) vs. introduces risk (single point of compromise)
  • Practice configuring a test SSO connection using a free IdP (e.g. Okta developer account or Azure AD trial)
  • Study federation trust relationships between organizations and common misconfiguration risks
  • Learn how token-based authentication (JWTs) fits into modern API/app authentication flows
  • Build a personal comparison chart: SAML vs OAuth2 vs OIDC — use case, actors, and trust model
SAML 2.0 OAuth2/OIDC Kerberos SSO

Task heading at the end of week: Test SSO Configuration

Configure a working SAML or OIDC SSO connection between a free IdP trial and a test application, and document the trust relationship and assertion flow.

Week 4: Access Control Models & MFA

  • Study Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): roles, permissions, and role-to-user assignment
  • Learn Attribute-Based Access Control (ABAC) and when it's used instead of/alongside RBAC
  • Study segregation of duties (SoD) and how to design roles that avoid toxic permission combinations
  • Learn multi-factor authentication (MFA) methods: TOTP, push notification, FIDO2/WebAuthn, SMS (and its weaknesses)
  • Practice setting up MFA on a test account using an authenticator app and reviewing the enrollment flow
  • Study conditional access policies: how risk-based signals (location, device) can modify auth requirements
  • Learn how privileged access differs from standard access and why it needs extra controls
  • Document a role design for a fictional 4-role organization, mapping permissions and flagging SoD conflicts
RBAC/ABAC MFA/FIDO2 Conditional Access SoD

Task heading at the end of week: Role Design & SoD Matrix

Design an RBAC role matrix for a fictional 4-role organization, mapping each role's permissions and identifying 2 segregation-of-duties conflicts to resolve.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You understand the identity lifecycle, can build and manage a directory service lab, configure federation/SSO, and design RBAC roles with proper segregation of duties — the foundational toolkit of an IAM Analyst.

Phase 2: Access Governance, IGA & Privileged Access

Days 31-60: Move from identity mechanics into governance — provisioning, access reviews, IGA tooling, and privileged access management

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Identity Governance (IGA) Access Reviews Privileged Access (PAM) Provisioning/SCIM Joiner-Mover-Leaver

Week 5: Identity Governance & Administration (IGA)

  • Study what IGA platforms actually do: centralizing identity governance across many connected systems
  • Explore a free trial/demo of an IGA tool (SailPoint, Saviynt, or Microsoft Entra ID Governance)
  • Learn how access certification campaigns work: who reviews what, how often, and why
  • Study how IGA platforms model entitlements and roles across disparate connected applications
  • Learn the concept of "toxic combinations" and how IGA tools automatically flag SoD violations
  • Practice mapping a fictional company's applications to an entitlement catalog structure
  • Study how IGA reporting feeds directly into compliance audits (tying back to GRC concepts)
  • Document how an access certification campaign would be scoped for a 100-person org
SailPoint/Saviynt Access Certification Entitlement Catalog Toxic Combinations

Task heading at the end of week: Access Certification Campaign Design

Design a full access certification campaign for a fictional 100-person org: scope, reviewer assignment, cadence, and escalation process for non-response.

Week 6: Provisioning, Deprovisioning & SCIM

  • Study automated provisioning: how a new hire in HR systems triggers downstream account creation
  • Learn SCIM (System for Cross-domain Identity Management) as the standard protocol for automated provisioning
  • Practice tracing a fictional joiner workflow end-to-end: HRIS trigger → IGA → target app account creation
  • Study why deprovisioning delays are one of the highest-risk gaps in real organizations
  • Learn how orphaned and dormant accounts get detected (and why they're a recurring audit finding)
  • Practice building a simple "leaver checklist" covering every system an employee might have access to
  • Study just-in-time (JIT) provisioning as an alternative to standing access
  • Document a full JML workflow diagram for a fictional mid-size company
SCIM Automated Provisioning Orphaned Accounts JIT Access

Task heading at the end of week: JML Workflow Diagram

Build a full joiner-mover-leaver workflow diagram for a fictional mid-size company, including every system touched and the deprovisioning SLA for each.

Week 7: Privileged Access Management (PAM)

  • Study why privileged accounts (admins, service accounts, break-glass accounts) need separate controls from standard access
  • Learn PAM core concepts: credential vaulting, session recording, just-in-time privilege elevation
  • Explore a free trial/demo of a PAM tool (CyberArk, BeyondTrust, or HashiCorp Boundary)
  • Study how privileged session monitoring and recording supports both security and audit needs
  • Learn the concept of "zero standing privilege" and how it differs from traditional always-on admin access
  • Practice designing a privileged access workflow: request, approval, time-boxed elevation, automatic revocation
  • Study service account management challenges: rotation, ownership, and the common "nobody owns this" problem
  • Document a PAM policy outline covering vaulting, session recording, and approval workflows
PAM Credential Vaulting Zero Standing Privilege Session Recording

Task heading at the end of week: Privileged Access Workflow Design

Design a complete just-in-time privileged access workflow for a fictional admin role, including request, approval, time-boxed elevation, and automatic revocation steps.

Week 8: Access Reviews & Audit Support

  • Learn how periodic access reviews work in practice: pulling reports, distributing to managers, tracking responses
  • Practice running a mock quarterly access review for a fictional 20-user, 5-system environment
  • Study how to detect and document excessive/stale access during a review cycle
  • Learn how IAM audit evidence gets prepared for external auditors (SOC 2, ISO 27001 control testing)
  • Practice writing an access review summary report with findings and remediation actions
  • Study how to handle manager non-response during a review (escalation paths, default actions)
  • Learn how access review metrics (completion rate, revocation rate) get tracked over time
  • Build a reusable access review tracker template you could apply to a real organization
Access Reviews Audit Evidence Stale Access Detection Review Metrics

Task heading at the end of week: Mock Access Review Cycle

Run a complete mock quarterly access review for a fictional 20-user environment, producing a findings summary and a remediation tracker for flagged access.

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You can design access certification campaigns, map full JML provisioning workflows, build privileged access controls, and run a complete access review cycle — the practical toolkit that fills an IAM Analyst's day-to-day work.

Phase 3: Identity Security, Reporting & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Identity threat detection, executive reporting, certification prep, and the job-search push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Identity Threat Detection IAM Metrics & Reporting Cloud IAM Awareness Cert Prep Interview Readiness

Week 9: Identity Threat Detection & Response

  • Study identity threat detection and response (ITDR) as an emerging IAM-adjacent discipline
  • Learn common identity attack patterns: credential stuffing, password spraying, MFA fatigue/push bombing
  • Study impossible travel and anomalous sign-in detection logic used in real identity platforms
  • Practice reviewing sample sign-in logs (Azure AD/Entra ID sign-in logs format) to spot suspicious patterns
  • Learn how conditional access policies serve as both prevention and detection-adjacent controls
  • Study how compromised credential alerts typically get triaged and escalated to a SOC
  • Practice writing a one-page incident response runbook for a "suspected account compromise" scenario
  • Document how identity logs would feed into a broader SIEM/SOC workflow
ITDR Anomalous Sign-In Detection Push Bombing/MFA Fatigue Conditional Access

Task heading at the end of week: Account Compromise Runbook

Write a complete incident response runbook for a suspected account compromise, covering detection signals, immediate containment steps, and escalation path.

Week 10: Cloud IAM & Multi-System Identity Awareness

  • Study how cloud provider IAM (AWS IAM, Azure RBAC, GCP IAM) relates to and differs from enterprise IAM/IGA
  • Learn how hybrid identity works: syncing on-prem AD to a cloud directory (Azure AD Connect concept)
  • Practice tracing how a single user identity flows from HR system → AD → cloud directory → SaaS apps
  • Study identity federation across SaaS applications and the role of an identity provider as the hub
  • Learn how API/service-to-service identity (machine identity, workload identity) differs from human identity
  • Study how non-human identity sprawl (API keys, service accounts) is becoming a major IAM governance gap
  • Practice mapping a fictional company's full identity ecosystem across on-prem, cloud, and SaaS
  • Document a "single identity, many systems" diagram showing the full chain for one sample user
Hybrid Identity SaaS Federation Machine Identity Identity Sprawl

Task heading at the end of week: Full Identity Ecosystem Map

Build a complete diagram tracing one fictional user's identity from HR system through AD, cloud directory, and 3 SaaS apps, noting the trust relationship at each hop.

Week 11: IAM Metrics, Reporting & Stakeholder Communication

  • Learn how to translate IAM program health into metrics leadership actually cares about
  • Study key IAM metrics: time-to-provision, time-to-deprovision, % of access reviews completed on time, orphaned account count
  • Practice building a simple IAM program dashboard summarizing these metrics for a fictional quarter
  • Learn how to write an executive summary translating a messy access review into a clear "here's our risk posture" statement
  • Study how IAM program gaps typically get framed as audit findings, and how to write a remediation plan for one
  • Practice presenting one IAM finding (e.g. deprovisioning delays) in plain, board-ready language
  • Learn how IAM roadmaps get prioritized: quick wins vs. platform investments vs. process fixes
  • Compile your 90-day labs into a single portfolio-style master IAM program report
IAM Metrics Executive Reporting Audit Findings Program Roadmaps

Task heading at the end of week: Master IAM Program Report

Compile your strongest artifacts from all 90 days (role matrix, JML workflow, access review, PAM policy) into one polished IAM program report with metrics and an executive summary.

Week 12: Certification & Interview Readiness

  • Review and attempt practice questions aligned to a target cert (e.g. CompTIA Security+, SailPoint IdentityNow Engineer, or CIAM-related certs)
  • Do a full mock IAM program review under time pressure: assess a fictional company's access controls and report within 4 hours
  • Prepare answers for common IAM interview questions (explain JML, walk through an access review, explain SAML vs OIDC, etc.)
  • Build a one-page resume bullet list translating your 90 days into measurable skills/achievements
  • Compile your strongest artifacts (role matrix, JML diagram, PAM policy, program report) into one portfolio
  • Practice a mock technical interview (explain identity federation to a non-technical hiring manager)
  • Research target companies/industries (finance, healthcare, SaaS) and tailor your resume accordingly
  • Plan your next 90 days: pick a specialization (IGA platform engineering, PAM, or identity security/ITDR)
Mock Interviews Resume Building Portfolio Building Cert Prep

Task heading at the end of week: Job-Ready IAM Portfolio

Finalize a portfolio folder (role matrix, JML diagram, access review, PAM policy, IAM program report), resume bullets, and a 2-minute mock interview recording.

You now have a working IAM toolkit (identity lifecycle, directory services, federation, access governance, privileged access, identity threat detection, program reporting) plus a portfolio of real artifacts and a job-ready application package — the foundation of a genuinely employable IAM Analyst.

What Next

Where to take your IAM career after Day 90

1. Specialize

  • Pick one lane: identity governance (IGA), privileged access (PAM), or identity security/ITDR
  • Go deep on one IGA platform (SailPoint, Saviynt, or Entra ID Governance) as a specialization
  • Consider PAM engineering (CyberArk/BeyondTrust) as a technical-adjacent niche
  • Explore identity architecture as a longer-term path
  • Look into non-human/machine identity governance as an emerging growth area
  • Consider customer IAM (CIAM) as a different specialization track
  • Specialize in one industry vertical (healthcare, finance, SaaS) for IAM compliance depth
  • Pursue an advanced cert aligned to your chosen lane
  • Join a specialized community for that niche
  • Set a 6-month deep-specialization goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to entry-level IAM Analyst / Identity Analyst roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description using your portfolio artifacts
  • Attend local ISSA/ISACA meetups and introduce yourself
  • Reach out to IAM professionals on LinkedIn for informational chats
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio folder updated with new artifacts
  • Practice mock interviews monthly to stay sharp
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider contract/temp IAM support gigs to build experience
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly identity security advisories and breach roundups
  • Re-test old AD labs and access review templates with new knowledge quarterly
  • Write a monthly post about something you learned in IAM
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio project every 90 days
  • Track major IAM platform updates and new ITDR capabilities
  • Mentor beginners — teaching reinforces your own mastery
  • Read one new IAM/identity security book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference or webinar per month
  • Get hands-on with a new IGA/PAM platform trial periodically
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create a beginner-friendly IAM series for your audience
  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (role matrix, JML diagram) for others to use
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on one IAM topic
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly
  • Pair up with another learner for accountability
  • Contribute feedback or templates to a community resource hub
  • Review and give feedback on a peer's role matrix or access review
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone starting out

Remember: IAM is the discipline of answering one question precisely, for every identity, at every moment: "should this access still exist?" Every role, review, and workflow you build exists to keep that answer current. Stay precise, document everything, and never let access outlive its reason for existing.
