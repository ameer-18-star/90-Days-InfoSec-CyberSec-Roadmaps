90-Day Roadmap For "Purple Team Lead" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for offense-and-defense-literate practitioners ready to own the integration itself — designing structured exercises, facilitating real-time collaboration between attackers and defenders, and running continuous validation as a permanent program rather than a one-time event.

Phase 1: Purple Team Program Design & Exercise Architecture

Days 1-30: Building the structured framework that turns ad hoc red/blue collaboration into a repeatable, measurable discipline

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Purple Team Methodology Exercise Design Bidirectional Fluency ATT&CK as Shared Language Program Cadence

Week 1: The Purple Team Lead Role & What Makes It Distinct

  • Study how purple teaming as a discipline differs from "a red team that talks to the blue team afterward": it's a structured methodology with its own cadence, artifacts, and success criteria
  • Learn the core purple team frameworks (MITRE's purple teaming guidance, TIBER-EU/CBEST-style threat-led testing concepts) and how they formalize collaboration
  • Study why a dedicated purple team lead role exists at all: pure red teams optimize for stealth, pure blue teams optimize for coverage, and someone needs to optimize for the transfer of knowledge between them
  • Learn how a purple team lead needs genuine bidirectional fluency — not expert-level in both offense and defense, but credible enough in each to earn trust from both sides
  • Study how to position the purple function organizationally: embedded within one team, a standalone function, or a rotating responsibility
  • Review one real public purple team program writeup or framework document and evaluate how it structures the red/blue interface
  • Learn how to navigate the trust dynamics — red teams worry purple "softens" their work, blue teams worry it's red team marketing with extra steps
  • Document a personal charter for your purple team function: scope, authority, and how it relates to existing red and blue teams

Week 2: ATT&CK as the Shared Operating Language

  • Study how MITRE ATT&CK serves as the common vocabulary that lets red and blue teams discuss the same activity in the same terms
  • Learn how to translate between red team operational language (tradecraft, technique names) and blue team detection language (data sources, log fields) using ATT&CK as the bridge
  • Practice building an ATT&CK technique-to-data-source mapping for 10 common techniques, showing exactly what telemetry would need to exist to detect each
  • Study how a purple lead uses ATT&CK Navigator to visualize both offensive test coverage and defensive detection coverage on the same map simultaneously
  • Learn how technique sub-categorization (procedures, not just techniques) matters — the same technique executed two different ways can have very different detection outcomes
  • Practice documenting 3 different procedural variations of one technique and how each would evade or trigger different detection logic
  • Study how to keep an organization's combined offense/defense ATT&CK map current as both red findings and blue detections evolve
  • Document a combined offense/defense ATT&CK heatmap template ready for ongoing program use
ATT&CK Translation Technique-to-Data-Source Mapping Procedural Variation Combined Heatmapping

Task heading at the end of week: Combined Offense/Defense ATT&CK Heatmap

Week 3: Structured Exercise Design

  • Study the standard purple team exercise structure: scoping, technique selection, execution, real-time observation, and joint debrief
  • Learn how to select techniques for an exercise based on threat relevance (tied to CTI) rather than whatever's easiest to demonstrate
  • Practice designing a full exercise plan selecting 8-10 techniques from one threat actor's known TTPs, sequenced logically across a kill chain
  • Study how to write an exercise plan that specifies exactly what "detected" and "not detected" mean for each technique before execution starts
  • Learn how to plan exercise timing and pacing so the blue team has genuine time to investigate each technique rather than rushing through a checklist
  • Practice writing pre-defined success criteria and expected detection outcomes for each technique in your exercise plan
  • Study how exercise scope should evolve over a program's life — starting with well-understood techniques and progressing to genuinely novel ones
  • Document a full structured exercise plan template covering scoping through success criteria definition

Week 4: Program Cadence & Continuous Validation Strategy

  • Study how purple teaming shifts from episodic (quarterly big exercises) toward continuous (smaller, frequent validation cycles) as a program matures
  • Learn how breach-and-attack simulation (BAS) platforms and Atomic Red Team enable lightweight, frequent validation between full collaborative exercises
  • Practice designing a tiered program cadence: weekly automated validation, monthly facilitated mini-exercises, quarterly full collaborative exercises
  • Study how to decide which techniques deserve automated continuous testing vs. which require genuine human-facilitated collaboration
  • Learn how regression testing works in a purple team context — re-validating that a previously-fixed detection gap hasn't silently broken again
  • Practice building a regression testing plan that re-runs your Phase 1 Week 2 techniques on a recurring schedule
  • Study how program cadence needs to flex around major organizational changes (new tooling, infrastructure migrations, staff turnover)
  • Document a full tiered program cadence design balancing automated and human-facilitated validation

You can charter a purple team function with real organizational positioning, use ATT&CK as a genuine translation layer between offense and defense, design structured exercises with pre-defined success criteria, and build a sustainable program cadence — the foundational toolkit of a Purple Team Lead.

Phase 2: Live Facilitation & Bidirectional Technical Credibility

Days 31-60: Running exercises in real time and maintaining enough hands-on depth in both disciplines to be trusted by both sides

🎯 Skills Goals

Live Exercise Facilitation Hands-On Technique Execution Detection Engineering Collaboration Conflict Mediation Real-Time Documentation

Week 5: Live Exercise Facilitation

  • Study the facilitator's core skill: guiding the blue team toward their own detection insight rather than simply announcing what happened
  • Learn how to pace a live exercise — pausing at the right moment after a technique executes, not too early (no time to investigate) or too late (momentum lost)
  • Practice running a mock live exercise checkpoint, walking a "blue team" through investigating one technique with guided questions rather than direct answers
  • Study how to handle the moment a technique goes fully undetected and the room goes quiet — keeping the session constructive, not deflating
  • Learn how to manage time across a multi-technique session so every planned technique gets a fair checkpoint, not just the first few
  • Practice facilitating a full mock exercise session covering 3 techniques end to end with a peer playing both red and blue roles
  • Study how to capture real-time decisions and insights during the session without the documentation burden disrupting the flow
  • Document a personal live facilitation playbook covering pacing, guided questioning technique, and momentum management

Week 6: Maintaining Hands-On Offensive Credibility

  • Study why a purple lead must personally execute techniques sometimes, not just direct others — credibility with red teams depends on demonstrated skill
  • Learn how to keep current with offensive tradecraft (C2 frameworks, evasion technique trends) without needing to be the most elite operator in the room
  • Practice personally executing 3 distinct ATT&CK techniques in a lab environment, documenting your execution as you would for a live exercise
  • Study how to select techniques you'll personally run during exercises vs. ones you'll direct an embedded red team operator to run
  • Learn how staying hands-on (even at a lighter cadence than a full-time operator) keeps your translation between red and blue genuinely credible rather than theoretical
  • Practice building one custom Atomic Red Team-style test for a technique not already covered by existing public tests
  • Study how to balance time spent staying technically sharp against the program-management and facilitation responsibilities of the role
  • Document a personal technical currency plan: how you'll keep hands-on skill from atrophying while leading the program

Week 7: Detection Engineering Collaboration in Real Time

  • Study how a purple lead works alongside detection engineers during an exercise to draft a rule's first version while the technique is still fresh
  • Learn how to translate a just-observed technique's behavioral indicators directly into a Sigma rule skeleton in real time
  • Practice writing a Sigma rule draft immediately following a mock technique execution, capturing the specific behavioral signature observed
  • Study how to validate a freshly-written rule against the same technique before the exercise session ends, closing the loop within the same sitting
  • Learn how to hand off a rule draft to the detection engineering team for proper testing/tuning without losing the context captured live
  • Practice writing a handoff document that preserves exercise context for a detection engineer who wasn't in the room
  • Study how same-session rule validation differs from (and complements) the formal detection engineering pipeline from a blue team's broader program
  • Document a real-time rule-drafting and handoff process for use during future live exercises

Week 8: Conflict Mediation & Maintaining Both Teams' Trust

  • Study common friction points: red teams feeling "outed" by detailed real-time disclosure, blue teams feeling blindsided or embarrassed by gaps
  • Learn how to mediate a moment where a red team operator and blue team analyst disagree about whether something was "really" detected
  • Practice a mock mediation scenario where a red operator insists a technique was stealthy and a blue analyst insists it was logged but not actioned
  • Study how to protect psychological safety on both sides — neither team should leave a session feeling humiliated
  • Learn how to set and enforce session ground rules upfront that prevent blame-oriented language before it starts
  • Practice writing a set of session ground rules you'd present at the start of every exercise
  • Study how repeated successful sessions build enough trust that both teams start volunteering information proactively rather than being drawn out
  • Document a conflict mediation playbook and a standard session ground-rules statement for ongoing program use

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Purple Program Metrics Coverage Improvement Tracking Program Scaling Executive Reporting Interview Readiness

Week 9: Purple Program Metrics That Prove Value

  • Study what genuinely demonstrates purple team value: closed-loop conversion rate (techniques tested → gaps found → detections built → re-validated), not just exercise count
  • Learn how to track coverage improvement over time on your combined ATT&CK heatmap, showing the trend from program start through now
  • Practice building a purple program metrics dashboard tracking technique coverage growth, mean time to close a detected gap, and regression pass rate

  • Learn how to report a session where nothing new was found as a genuine success (confirmed existing coverage holds) rather than a wasted exercise
  • Practice writing a quarterly purple program report combining metrics, narrative highlights, and next-quarter priorities
  • Study how purple program metrics should feed into both the red team's and blue team's own separate program metrics, not exist in isolation
  • Document a full purple program metrics framework and one sample quarterly report

What Next

Where to take your purple team career after Day 90

1. Specialize or Broaden

  • Decide: deepen toward continuous validation/BAS engineering, or broaden toward enterprise integration program leadership
  • Consider a Continuous Validation Specialist track if BAS tooling and automated regression testing energized you most
  • Consider an Enterprise Integration Lead track if program scaling and champion network building stood out
  • Explore a return to dedicated Red or Blue Team Lead if one discipline pulls you back toward deeper specialization
  • Look into independent purple team consulting once your reputation across both disciplines is strong enough
  • Consider specializing in a high-stakes industry vertical (critical infrastructure, finance, defense)
  • Pursue advanced offensive certs (OSEP, CRTL) if maintaining red-side depth is the priority
  • Pursue advanced defensive certs (GDSA, GCDA) if maintaining blue-side depth is the priority
  • Join a specialized community for your chosen direction
  • Set a 6-12 month direction-setting goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Purple Team Lead / Detection & Adversary Emulation Lead / Security Integration Lead roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description, emphasizing bidirectional fluency and facilitation leadership
  • Attend local BSides/DEF CON purple team village meetups

  • Keep your portfolio updated with new exercise artifacts and program metrics
  • Practice behavioral (STAR-format) mock interviews monthly
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider organizations with already-mature red AND blue functions, since purple teaming requires both to exist meaningfully

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly developments across both offensive tradecraft and detection engineering

  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio artifact every 90 days
  • Track new BAS platform and detection engineering tooling developments
  • Mentor both red and blue practitioners moving toward purple readiness — teaching reinforces your own bidirectional mastery
  • Read one new offensive and one new defensive book each quarter to maintain genuine balance
  • Attend at least one virtual conference talk per month
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

Remember: Purple isn't a compromise between red and blue, it's a discipline in its own right — the deliberate practice of turning "we got past them" and "we didn't see it" into "we now see it, and we proved it." Every exercise, checkpoint, and metric you build exists to answer one question: "did this session leave the organization measurably harder to compromise, and can both teams point to exactly why?" Stay credible on both sides, stay fair to both teams, and always close the loop from technique to validated detection.
