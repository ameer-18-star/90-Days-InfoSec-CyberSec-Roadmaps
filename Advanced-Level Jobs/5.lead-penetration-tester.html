90-Day Roadmap For "Lead Penetration Tester" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for testers who've already run individual engagements and are ready to lead them — scoping the work, directing a team through complex attack chains, and owning the client relationship from kickoff to debrief.

Phase 1: Engagement Leadership & Advanced Exploitation

Days 1-30: Scoping, methodology ownership, and directing the technical depth of a real engagement

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Engagement Scoping Rules of Engagement Active Directory Exploitation Methodology Frameworks Team Direction

Week 1: The Lead Tester Role & Engagement Scoping

  • Study what changes between "running an engagement" and "leading one": you now own scope, risk, and client expectations, not just exploitation
  • Learn how to run a scoping call: clarifying objectives, in-scope/out-of-scope assets, testing windows, and acceptable techniques
  • Study the difference between a vulnerability assessment, a penetration test, and a red team engagement — and how to recommend the right one
  • Learn how to write a Statement of Work (SOW) that protects both the client and the testing team from scope ambiguity
  • Study how to estimate engagement effort/duration realistically based on scope size and complexity
  • Review one real public pentest report's scope/methodology section and evaluate how clearly it was defined
  • Learn how to handle a scope-creep request mid-engagement without damaging the client relationship
  • Document a reusable scoping questionnaire and SOW template for your own future engagements
Scoping Calls Statement of Work VA vs PT vs Red Team Effort Estimation

Task heading at the end of week: Scoping Questionnaire & SOW Template

Build a complete scoping questionnaire and a Statement of Work template, then use both to scope one fictional engagement end to end, including an effort estimate.

Week 2: Rules of Engagement & Legal/Operational Safety

  • Study what belongs in a Rules of Engagement (RoE) document: authorized techniques, emergency contacts, blackout windows, escalation procedures
  • Learn how to negotiate testing constraints with a client who's nervous about production system impact
  • Study "get out of jail free" letters/authorization documentation and why a lead must personally verify they're signed before testing begins
  • Learn how to plan for and communicate an emergency stop procedure if testing causes unexpected impact
  • Study how to handle discovering an active, unrelated compromise mid-engagement (a real incident, not a finding)
  • Practice writing a full RoE document for a fictional engagement involving production and non-production environments
  • Study how data handling rules (what you can exfiltrate as proof, how you store findings) protect both parties
  • Document a personal pre-engagement legal/safety checklist you'll run through before every test begins
Rules of Engagement Authorization Letters Emergency Stop Procedures Data Handling

Task heading at the end of week: Rules of Engagement Document

Write a complete Rules of Engagement document for a fictional engagement, including authorized techniques, emergency stop procedure, and a pre-engagement legal/safety checklist.

Week 3: Advanced Active Directory & Internal Network Exploitation

  • Study advanced AD attack paths: Kerberoasting, ASREPRoasting, delegation abuse (unconstrained/constrained/RBCD), DCSync
  • Practice running a full AD attack chain in a lab (e.g. GOAD or a self-built vulnerable domain) from initial foothold to domain admin
  • Learn how BloodHound is used to map attack paths and identify the highest-value escalation routes in a real environment
  • Study lateral movement techniques: pass-the-hash, pass-the-ticket, WMI/PsExec/WinRM-based movement
  • Learn how a lead decides which attack path to pursue when multiple viable paths exist, weighing stealth, time, and client risk
  • Practice documenting a full attack chain narrative as you'd present it in a client debrief, not just a list of commands run
  • Study how to recognize and work around common EDR/AV detection during an authorized internal engagement
  • Write a full internal network engagement report section covering one complete attack chain from foothold to domain compromise
BloodHound Kerberoasting/DCSync Lateral Movement GOAD Lab

Task heading at the end of week: Full AD Attack Chain Report

Run a complete attack chain in a lab AD environment from initial foothold to domain admin, and write the report section as you'd present it in a real client debrief.

Week 4: Methodology Ownership & Team Technical Direction

  • Study established methodology frameworks (PTES, OWASP Testing Guide, OSSTMM) and how a lead selects/adapts one per engagement
  • Learn how a lead allocates team members across attack surfaces (external, internal, web app, wireless) for efficient coverage
  • Practice writing a testing plan that sequences activities logically (recon before exploitation, low-noise before loud) for a multi-person team
  • Study how to review a junior tester's findings for accuracy and exploitability before they go into a report
  • Learn how to recognize when a team member is stuck on a path and redirect them without micromanaging
  • Practice a mock daily standup: 2-3 testers report progress, you synthesize and reprioritize the remaining engagement time
  • Study how to track engagement progress against the agreed scope and timeline, flagging risk of running over
  • Document a reusable engagement testing plan template and a finding-review checklist for quality control

Task heading at the end of week: Engagement Testing Plan & QA Checklist

Build a complete testing plan sequencing activities for a multi-person team across one fictional engagement, plus a finding-review checklist for quality control.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can scope an engagement properly, write a defensible Rules of Engagement, run a full advanced AD attack chain, and direct a team's technical work across a sequenced testing plan — the foundational toolkit of a Lead Penetration Tester.

Phase 2: Advanced Exploitation Depth & Client Relationship Management

Days 31-60: Pushing technical depth across web/cloud/AD, and owning the client relationship through findings and pushback

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Advanced Web Exploitation Cloud Penetration Testing Client Communication Risk-Rating Disputes Adversary Emulation

Week 5: Advanced Web Application & API Exploitation

  • Study advanced web exploitation beyond OWASP Top 10 basics: SSRF chaining into cloud metadata, deserialization RCE, request smuggling
  • Practice chaining a moderate-severity finding (e.g. SSRF) into a critical-impact outcome (cloud credential theft) in a lab
  • Learn advanced authentication bypass techniques: JWT algorithm confusion, OAuth flow manipulation, race conditions in auth logic
  • Study GraphQL-specific advanced attacks: batching abuse, introspection-driven schema exploitation
  • Practice a full API penetration test on a complex sample target (crAPI or similar) finding and chaining multiple findings together
  • Learn how a lead decides when to stop chaining (sufficient business impact demonstrated) vs. continue for completeness
  • Study how to write an impact statement that makes a technical chain land with a non-technical executive reader
  • Write a full exploit chain narrative connecting 2-3 individually moderate findings into one critical business risk story

Task heading at the end of week: Critical Exploit Chain Narrative

Build and document a full exploit chain connecting 2-3 individually moderate web/API findings into one demonstrated critical business impact, written for a client report.

Week 6: Cloud Penetration Testing

  • Study how cloud penetration testing differs from traditional network/AD testing: API-driven attack surface, IAM as the new perimeter
  • Practice using Pacu or similar tooling to enumerate and exploit AWS IAM misconfigurations in a sandbox account
  • Learn how privilege escalation paths in cloud IAM (overly permissive roles, iam:PassRole abuse) get identified and chained
  • Study container/Kubernetes-specific penetration testing: escaping a container, abusing exposed kubelet APIs, RBAC misconfigurations
  • Practice a guided cloud pentest scenario (e.g. CloudGoat) achieving a defined objective like data exfiltration or privilege escalation
  • Learn how cloud provider rules of engagement differ (AWS/Azure/GCP penetration testing policies) and what's authorized by default
  • Study how to present cloud findings in business terms when the client's technical team may not fully understand cloud-native risk yet
  • Write a full cloud penetration test report section documenting one complete IAM privilege escalation chain

Task heading at the end of week: Cloud IAM Escalation Report

Week 7: Client Communication & Managing Pushback

  • Study how to run an effective mid-engagement check-in call: surfacing critical findings early without waiting for the final report
  • Learn how to deliver a critical finding verbally with appropriate urgency without causing panic or appearing alarmist
  • Practice a mock call notifying a client of a critical finding discovered mid-engagement, requiring immediate attention
  • Study how to handle a client who disputes a finding's severity or claims "that's not really exploitable in our environment"
  • Learn how to defend a risk rating with evidence while remaining open to genuinely new context that should adjust it
  • Practice a mock pushback conversation where a client's engineering lead disputes your CVSS scoring
  • Study how to manage a client relationship across multiple repeat engagements (annual pentests, ongoing retainers)
  • Document a client communication playbook covering check-ins, critical notifications, and dispute-handling scripts
Mid-Engagement Check-ins Critical Notifications Severity Disputes Repeat Client Relationships

Task heading at the end of week: Client Communication Playbook

Write a complete client communication playbook covering mid-engagement check-ins, a critical-finding notification script, and a severity-dispute handling approach.

Week 8: Adversary Emulation & Red Team-Adjacent Engagements

  • Study how adversary emulation/red team engagements differ from standard penetration tests: objective-based, stealth-focused, broader scope
  • Learn how to design a red-team-style engagement around a specific threat actor's known TTPs (mapped to MITRE ATT&CK)
  • Practice using a C2 framework (Sliver, Mythic, or Cobalt Strike trial/community edition) in a fully isolated lab for command and control
  • Study OPSEC considerations for red team work: minimizing detection footprint, covering tracks appropriately within RoE
  • Learn how purple team collaboration works when a red team engagement is paired with the client's defenders for mutual learning
  • Practice planning a red-team-style engagement scenario mapped to a specific threat actor profile relevant to a client's industry
  • Study how engagement objectives differ (e.g. "reach the crown-jewel database" vs. "test detection capability") and how that changes tactics
  • Document a full adversary emulation engagement plan including threat actor selection, objectives, and success criteria

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You can chain findings into demonstrated business-critical impact across web and cloud surfaces, manage client communication through pushback and critical notifications, and plan an adversary emulation engagement — the technical-and-relational toolkit that defines a Lead Penetration Tester.

Phase 3: Reporting Excellence, Practice Building & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Reports that get fixes prioritized, building a pentest practice, and the certification/interview push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Executive-Ready Reporting Pentest Practice Building Quality Assurance Retesting & Verification Interview Readiness

Week 9: Reporting Excellence & Executive Summaries

  • Study what separates a report that drives remediation from one that gets filed and forgotten
  • Learn how to write an executive summary that a CISO can act on in 5 minutes without reading a single technical finding
  • Practice writing an executive summary for a complex multi-finding engagement, leading with business risk, not CVE counts
  • Study how to structure findings for maximum clarity: clear title, business impact, technical detail, proof, remediation, references
  • Learn how visual aids (attack path diagrams, network maps) make a report dramatically more persuasive than text alone
  • Practice building one attack path diagram for your Phase 2 critical chain finding
  • Study how to write remediation guidance that's specific enough to action, not generic "apply patches" advice
  • Compile a full polished report for one complete mock engagement, combining executive summary, findings, and visual aids
Executive Summaries Finding Structure Attack Path Diagrams Actionable Remediation

Task heading at the end of week: Polished Mock Engagement Report

Compile one complete, polished pentest report for a mock engagement, including an executive summary, well-structured findings, and at least one attack path diagram.

Week 10: Quality Assurance & Reviewing Others' Work

  • Study how a lead's QA review of a junior tester's report differs from writing one yourself: catching gaps, not redoing the work
  • Learn a structured report QA checklist: technical accuracy, evidence sufficiency, severity consistency, writing clarity
  • Practice reviewing a sample (deliberately flawed) report and identifying every issue using your checklist
  • Study how to give report feedback that improves a junior tester's skill, not just fixes the document in front of you
  • Learn how to calibrate severity ratings consistently across a team so the same vulnerability class gets the same rating regardless of who found it
  • Practice writing structured feedback comments on a flawed mock report, balancing thoroughness with not being discouraging
  • Study how a second-reviewer process (peer review before client delivery) catches errors a single author misses
  • Document a full report QA checklist and a feedback-delivery framework for your team's quality process
Report QA Severity Calibration Constructive Feedback Peer Review Process

Task heading at the end of week: Report QA Checklist & Review

Build a complete report QA checklist, then use it to review a deliberately flawed sample report and write structured, constructive feedback comments.

Week 11: Retesting, Verification & Pentest Practice Operations

  • Study how retesting/verification engagements work: confirming fixes without re-running a full engagement from scratch
  • Learn how to scope a retest efficiently, focusing only on previously identified findings plus a sanity check for regressions
  • Practice writing a retest report confirming remediation status for a set of mock prior findings (fixed/not fixed/partially fixed)
  • Study how a pentest practice tracks engagement metrics: utilization, finding trends across clients, repeat business rate
  • Learn how a lead contributes to practice-level decisions: tooling investment, certification requirements for the team, methodology updates
  • Practice writing a practice improvement proposal (e.g. adopting a new methodology element or tool) with a business justification
  • Study how to onboard a new tester into your team's specific methodology, tooling, and reporting standards quickly
  • Compile your 90-day labs into a single portfolio-style master engagement leadership package

Week 12: Certification & Interview Readiness

  • Review and attempt practice questions/labs aligned to a target cert (e.g. OSCP, CRTO, or GPEN)
  • Do a full mock engagement under time pressure: scope, test a lab target, and produce a client-ready report within a set window
  • Prepare answers for lead tester interview questions (walk through scoping a tricky engagement, describe handling a severity dispute, explain a critical chain you found)
  • Build a one-page resume bullet list translating your 90 days into measurable technical and leadership achievements
  • Polish your portfolio: pin your best 3 artifacts (pre-engagement package, critical chain report, client communication playbook) with clean documentation
  • Practice a mock behavioral interview using STAR format for your client pushback and team-direction stories
  • Research target consultancies/MSSPs and tailor your resume + LinkedIn headline to emphasize engagement leadership specifically
  • Plan your next 90 days: pick a direction (red team lead, pentest practice manager, or independent consulting)

What Next

Where to take your offensive security career after Day 90

1. Specialize or Broaden

  • Decide: deepen toward red team/adversary emulation, or broaden toward pentest practice management
  • Consider a Red Team Lead track if adversary emulation and stealth tradecraft energized you most
  • Consider a Pentest Practice Manager track if client relationships and team QA stood out
  • Explore independent consulting as a path once your reputation and network are strong enough
  • Look into specializing in cloud-native or IoT/OT penetration testing as growth areas
  • Consider a high-stakes industry vertical specialization (finance, critical infrastructure, healthcare)
  • Pursue OSEP/CRTO if the red team depth track is the clear direction
  • Pursue CISSP/PMP if the practice management track is the clear direction
  • Join a specialized community for your chosen direction
  • Set a 6-12 month direction-setting goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Lead Penetration Tester / Senior Pentest Consultant / Red Team Lead roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description, emphasizing engagement leadership and client-facing experience
  • Attend local BSides/DEF CON red team village meetups
  • Reach out to current lead testers and red team leads on LinkedIn for informational chats
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio updated with new exploit chains and engagement artifacts
  • Practice behavioral (STAR-format) mock interviews monthly
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider boutique pentest consultancies for varied, high-leadership-exposure engagements
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly exploit research and new attack technique disclosures
  • Do at least one new HackTheBox Pro Lab or advanced CTF per month to stay sharp
  • Re-run old AD/cloud labs with new techniques as tooling and TTPs evolve
  • Write a monthly technical blog post about something you learned
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio artifact every 90 days
  • Track new red team tooling and C2 framework developments
  • Mentor junior testers moving toward lead readiness — teaching reinforces your own mastery
  • Read one new offensive security book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference talk per month
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create a "becoming a lead tester" series for senior testers aspiring to lead
  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (RoE, testing plan, report template) for others to use
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on engagement leadership or exploit chaining
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly
  • Pair up with another aspiring lead for accountability
  • Contribute documentation fixes to a tool you use often
  • Review and give feedback on a peer's report or engagement plan
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone starting out

✅ Managed client communication through a critical notification and a severity dispute | ✅ Designed an adversary emulation engagement plan | ✅ Produced an executive-ready report, QA'd a peer's work, and completed a retest


Technologies Covered: BloodHound, Impacket, Mimikatz, Burp Suite Pro, Pacu, CloudGoat, Sliver/Mythic, Caldera, PlexTrac/Sysreptor, GOAD lab environment.


Remember: Leading an engagement isn't about finding the most vulnerabilities — it's about scoping the work honestly, directing a team toward the findings that actually matter, and telling the client a true, persuasive story about their risk. Every chain, report, and client call you lead exists to answer one question: "did this engagement make the client measurably safer, and will they trust us enough to call again next year?" Stay sharp, document everything, and always chain toward business impact.
