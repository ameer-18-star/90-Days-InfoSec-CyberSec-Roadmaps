90-Day Roadmap For "Senior Cyber Security" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for security professionals who've already built deep expertise in one or more domains and are ready to operate at the enterprise level — security strategy, cross-domain technical leadership, and the bridge between hands-on practitioner and security management.

Phase 1: Enterprise Security Architecture & Strategic Risk

Days 1-30: Thinking at the enterprise level — security architecture frameworks, defense-in-depth design, and strategic risk assessment

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Security Architecture Zero Trust Strategy Enterprise Risk Defense-in-Depth Security Frameworks

Week 1: Enterprise Security Thinking

  • Study what changes at the enterprise level: decisions now affect budget, headcount, and multi-year technical debt, not just one system
  • Learn how to read an organization holistically: business model, regulatory exposure, and how security risk maps to business risk
  • Study the SABSA framework's business-attribute-driven approach to security architecture
  • Learn how enterprise architecture (EA) and security architecture relate, and where security should have a seat at the table
  • Study how a Chief Information Security Officer's priorities cascade down into technical workstreams
  • Review one real public enterprise security strategy document or annual report security section, and identify its priorities
  • Learn how to translate a business objective ("expand into the EU market") into security implications (data residency, GDPR)
  • Document a one-page "business-to-security-risk" translation exercise for a fictional company's strategic initiative
SABSA Enterprise Architecture Business-Risk Translation Strategic Thinking

Task heading at the end of week: Business-to-Security Translation Brief

Write a one-page brief translating one fictional business expansion initiative into its concrete security and compliance implications, framed for a non-technical executive audience.

Week 2: Security Architecture Frameworks & Defense-in-Depth

  • Study defense-in-depth as an architectural philosophy, not just a checklist of overlapping controls
  • Learn how to design layered controls across network, identity, endpoint, application, and data layers cohesively
  • Practice mapping an existing (or fictional) enterprise's controls against a layered defense-in-depth model and identifying single points of failure
  • Study Zero Trust Architecture (NIST SP 800-207) as the modern evolution of perimeter-based defense-in-depth
  • Learn how micro-segmentation, continuous verification, and least privilege combine into a coherent Zero Trust strategy
  • Study how legacy infrastructure realistically migrates toward Zero Trust incrementally, not overnight
  • Practice designing a phased Zero Trust adoption roadmap for a fictional traditional enterprise network
  • Document a defense-in-depth reference architecture diagram spanning all major control layers
Defense-in-Depth NIST SP 800-207 Micro-Segmentation Zero Trust Roadmap

Task heading at the end of week: Zero Trust Adoption Roadmap

Design a phased, multi-quarter Zero Trust adoption roadmap for a fictional traditional enterprise network, including a defense-in-depth reference architecture diagram.

Week 3: Enterprise Risk Assessment & Threat Landscape Analysis

  • Study how enterprise risk assessment differs from system-level risk assessment: aggregate exposure, concentration risk, systemic dependencies
  • Learn how to build a threat landscape analysis specific to an industry vertical (e.g. financial services vs. healthcare vs. manufacturing)
  • Practice researching and writing a threat landscape brief for one industry, covering relevant threat actors, common attack patterns, and regulatory pressure
  • Study how geopolitical and supply chain risk factor into enterprise-level threat modeling (nation-state targeting, critical vendor concentration)
  • Learn how cyber risk quantification at the enterprise level (aggregate FAIR-style modeling) supports board-level risk appetite decisions
  • Practice presenting an aggregate risk picture (not just individual findings) for a fictional organization's top 5 systemic risks
  • Study how cyber insurance underwriting criteria are increasingly shaping enterprise security investment priorities
  • Document an industry threat landscape brief ready to inform a strategic security planning session
Enterprise Risk Threat Landscape Analysis Supply Chain Risk Aggregate Risk Modeling

Task heading at the end of week: Industry Threat Landscape Brief

Write a complete threat landscape brief for one industry vertical, covering relevant threat actors, attack patterns, regulatory pressure, and your top 5 systemic risk recommendations.

Week 4: Security Frameworks at Scale

  • Study how NIST CSF 2.0, ISO 27001, and CIS Controls get implemented and harmonized across a large, multi-business-unit organization
  • Learn how a unified control framework reduces audit fatigue when an organization faces multiple compliance obligations simultaneously
  • Practice building a cross-framework control mapping spanning 15+ controls across NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and SOC 2 for enterprise-scale reuse
  • Study how a security maturity model gets applied across business units that may be at very different maturity levels
  • Learn how mergers and acquisitions introduce sudden security architecture and framework integration challenges
  • Practice writing a high-level security due diligence checklist for evaluating an acquisition target's security posture
  • Study how a Security Governance Council or similar cross-functional body typically operates at the enterprise level
  • Document a unified control framework mapping spreadsheet ready for reuse across multiple compliance audits
NIST CSF/ISO 27001/CIS Framework Harmonization M&A Security Due Diligence Security Governance

Task heading at the end of week: Unified Control Framework Map

Build a cross-framework control mapping spanning 15+ controls across NIST CSF, ISO 27001, and SOC 2, plus a one-page M&A security due diligence checklist.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can translate business strategy into security requirements, design defense-in-depth and Zero Trust architectures at scale, assess enterprise-level systemic risk, and harmonize multiple compliance frameworks — the foundational toolkit of a senior cyber security leader.

Phase 2: Security Program Leadership & Crisis Management

Days 31-60: Leading security programs and people, and operating as the senior technical voice during major crises

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Program Leadership Crisis Management Vendor/Tool Strategy Security Culture at Scale Regulatory Engagement

Week 5: Security Program & Team Leadership

  • Study how a senior cyber security leader balances individual contribution with enabling a team's collective output
  • Learn how to structure a security team across functions (SOC, AppSec, GRC, IAM) and identify coverage gaps
  • Practice writing a team capability gap analysis for a fictional security organization
  • Study how hiring decisions, skills development, and career pathing fit into senior technical leadership responsibility
  • Learn how to run effective security team retrospectives focused on systemic improvement, not individual blame
  • Practice designing a quarterly OKR (Objectives and Key Results) set for a security team aligned to business priorities
  • Study how to balance "keep the lights on" operational work against strategic improvement initiatives in resourcing
  • Document a team structure and capability roadmap for a fictional 15-person security organization
Team Structure Capability Gap Analysis OKRs Retrospectives

Task heading at the end of week: Security Team Capability Roadmap

Build a complete team structure and capability gap analysis for a fictional 15-person security organization, plus one quarter's OKRs aligned to a stated business priority.

Week 6: Major Incident & Crisis Leadership

  • Study how senior leaders operate differently during a major incident than hands-on responders: decisions, not tasks
  • Learn how to structure a crisis management team spanning security, legal, communications, and executive leadership
  • Practice leading a mock board-level briefing during a simulated active ransomware incident
  • Study how regulatory breach notification obligations (GDPR's 72-hour rule, state breach laws) shape crisis timeline decisions
  • Learn how decisions about paying ransom, engaging law enforcement, or going public get made and who's typically involved
  • Practice writing a crisis communication statement for both internal staff and external customers during a breach scenario
  • Study how post-crisis trust rebuilding (with customers, regulators, the board) extends well beyond technical remediation
  • Write a full crisis leadership playbook for a major incident scenario, covering decision authority and communication flow
Crisis Leadership Breach Notification Law Board Briefings Crisis Communications

Task heading at the end of week: Crisis Leadership Playbook

Write a complete crisis leadership playbook for a major ransomware scenario, including decision authority structure, regulatory timeline obligations, and draft internal/external communications.

Week 7: Security Tooling Strategy & Vendor Management

  • Study how enterprise security tool stacks accumulate redundancy and gaps over time, and how to rationalize them
  • Learn how to evaluate build-vs-buy decisions for security capabilities at an enterprise scale
  • Practice writing a security tooling rationalization analysis for a fictional org with 20+ overlapping security tools
  • Study how to run a structured vendor evaluation (RFP-style) for a major security platform decision
  • Learn how total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis should factor into security tooling decisions, not just sticker price
  • Practice negotiating concepts for enterprise security contracts: multi-year terms, SLAs, exit clauses
  • Study how platform consolidation strategies (single vendor ecosystem vs. best-of-breed) trade off integration ease against lock-in risk
  • Document a tooling rationalization recommendation with a 12-month consolidation roadmap
Tool Rationalization Build vs Buy TCO Analysis Vendor Negotiation

Task heading at the end of week: Security Tooling Rationalization Plan

Write a complete tooling rationalization analysis for a fictional org's overlapping security tools, including a TCO comparison and a 12-month consolidation roadmap.

Week 8: Security Culture & Regulatory Engagement at Scale

  • Study how security culture initiatives scale differently across a 50-person company vs. a 5,000-person enterprise
  • Learn how to design a security champions network spanning multiple business units with executive sponsorship
  • Practice writing an executive sponsorship pitch for a cross-organization security culture initiative
  • Study how organizations engage proactively with regulators (rather than only reactively during incidents)
  • Learn how industry information-sharing bodies (ISACs) function and what value enterprise participation provides
  • Practice drafting talking points for a regulatory examination or audit interview as the senior security representative
  • Study how public disclosure expectations (SEC cyber disclosure rules, for example) shape ongoing security reporting obligations
  • Document a regulatory engagement and culture-scaling strategy for a fictional enterprise's next fiscal year
Security Culture at Scale Regulatory Engagement ISACs Disclosure Obligations

Task heading at the end of week: Culture & Regulatory Strategy

Write a complete security culture-scaling and regulatory engagement strategy for a fictional enterprise's next fiscal year, including an executive sponsorship pitch.

Phase 3: Strategic Influence, Board Engagement & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Long-term strategy, board-level communication, and the certification/interview push for senior cyber security leadership roles

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Board Communication Long-Term Strategy Emerging Tech Risk Personal Brand/Thought Leadership Interview Readiness

Week 9: Board-Level Communication & Governance

  • Study how board members typically think about cyber risk: fiduciary duty, reputational risk, not technical detail
  • Learn how to structure a board-level cyber risk presentation: 3-5 key messages, not a comprehensive technical report
  • Practice building a board-ready slide deck summarizing organizational cyber risk posture and key initiatives
  • Study how board cyber risk committees operate and what recurring questions they typically ask
  • Learn how to handle a skeptical or technically uninformed board member's pointed question gracefully and substantively
  • Practice a mock board presentation and Q&A session (recruit a peer to play a tough board member)
  • Study how director and officer (D&O) liability considerations are increasingly tied to demonstrable cyber governance
  • Document a board-ready annual cyber risk report for a fictional organization
Board Reporting Cyber Risk Committees D&O Liability Context Executive Communication

Task heading at the end of week: Board-Ready Annual Cyber Risk Report

Build a complete board-ready presentation summarizing a fictional organization's annual cyber risk posture, key initiatives, and 3-5 headline messages.

Week 10: Long-Term Strategy & Emerging Technology Risk

  • Study how to build a 3-year (not just quarterly) security strategy that survives changing tactical priorities
  • Learn how emerging technology adoption (AI/ML systems, IoT, quantum-readiness) introduces new categories of enterprise risk
  • Practice writing a risk assessment for one emerging technology your organization (real or fictional) is adopting
  • Study how AI governance and security (model risk, data poisoning, prompt injection at the enterprise level) is becoming a distinct discipline
  • Learn how to balance innovation enablement against risk introduction when new technology initiatives arrive at speed
  • Practice presenting a "yes, and here's how we do it safely" response to a leadership team eager to adopt new technology fast
  • Study how post-quantum cryptography planning is becoming a forward-looking strategic consideration for long-lived data
  • Document a 3-year security strategy outline incorporating at least one emerging technology risk consideration

Week 11: Thought Leadership & Industry Presence

  • Study why senior cyber security professionals increasingly build public thought leadership as part of their career capital
  • Learn how to write a substantive industry-facing article or analysis piece (not promotional content) on a security topic
  • Practice drafting one original thought leadership piece on a strategic security topic you've developed a real point of view on
  • Study how speaking at conferences, webinars, or industry panels builds both personal brand and organizational credibility
  • Learn how to craft a compelling conference talk abstract/pitch for a security leadership topic
  • Practice writing one conference talk abstract and a corresponding 10-minute outline
  • Study how mentoring and advisory roles (startup advisory boards, university guest lecturing) extend senior influence beyond one's own organization
  • Compile your 90-day strategic work into a single portfolio-style "senior cyber security leadership" writeup

  • Do a full mock "executive day" exercise: build a board slide, handle a crisis briefing scenario, and respond to a tooling budget question, all within a time limit
  • Prepare answers for senior leadership interview questions (describe a strategy you set, a crisis you led, a tradeoff between innovation and risk)
  • Build a one-page resume/bio translating your 90 days into measurable strategic impact and leadership signals

  • Practice a mock executive-level interview using STAR format for your crisis leadership and strategy-setting stories
  • Research target organizations and tailor your resume/LinkedIn headline to emphasize strategic and leadership breadth

What Next

Where to take your cyber security career after Day 90

1. Choose Your Direction

  • Decide: CISO/executive leadership track, principal/distinguished engineer track, or independent advisory/consulting
  • Consider a CISO track if board communication and strategy-setting energized you most
  • Consider a principal/distinguished engineer track if deep technical influence without people management appeals more
  • Explore vCISO/fractional advisory work as a flexible, portfolio-career path
  • Look into board advisory or director roles as a very long-term aspiration
  • Consider specializing further in one high-stakes vertical (critical infrastructure, finance, healthcare) at the leadership level
  • Pursue CCISO or executive education if the CISO track is the clear direction
  • Pursue SABSA practitioner-level credentials if architecture leadership is the clear direction
  • Join executive-level peer communities for your chosen direction
  • Set a 12-month direction-setting goal — this decision deserves real deliberation, not a rushed choice

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Senior Security Architect / Director of Security / Head of Security roles aligned to your direction
  • Tailor your resume/bio per opportunity, emphasizing strategic impact and leadership breadth
  • Attend executive-level ISSA/ISACA roundtables and CISO peer events
  • Reach out to current CISOs and senior security leaders for informational conversations
  • Ask for introductions once you've built genuine peer relationships
  • Keep your executive portfolio updated with new strategic artifacts
  • Practice executive-level mock interviews regularly, focused on judgment and strategy stories
  • Track conversations and follow up professionally and consistently
  • Consider advisory/fractional roles as a way to build a broader leadership track record
  • Build relationships steadily — senior roles are filled through networks more often than job boards

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly strategic security and geopolitical risk content
  • Revisit your 3-year strategy annually as a deliberate planning ritual
  • Write regularly about strategic judgment calls and lessons learned
  • Build a second, more advanced strategic artifact every quarter
  • Track emerging technology and regulatory shifts continuously
  • Mentor at least one rising senior analyst consistently
  • Read one new strategy/leadership book per month, not just technical ones
  • Attend executive-level conferences or roundtables regularly
  • Seek structured feedback on your executive communication and influence skills
  • Reassess your career direction annually, deliberately, with trusted peers or a coach

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create a "growing into senior leadership" series for rising senior analysts
  • Answer questions in community forums regularly, especially career-direction questions
  • Open-source one of your frameworks (crisis playbook template, strategy outline) for others to use
  • Host a workshop or webinar on enterprise security strategy or crisis leadership
  • Write a public "lessons learned" retrospective on your strategic growth
  • Formally mentor at least one senior analyst working toward leadership
  • Contribute to industry frameworks or community standards where you have real expertise
  • Speak publicly to extend access to senior-level thinking beyond your own organization
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone aiming for senior leadership

Remember: At this level, your job is no longer to be the best technician in the room — it's to make sure the right decisions get made, the right people are empowered to act, and the organization is measurably more resilient because you were the one steering. Every strategy, briefing, and mentoring conversation exists to answer one question: "if a crisis hit tomorrow, would this organization be ready, and would it trust me to lead through it?" Stay curious, communicate with clarity, and keep thinking three years ahead.
