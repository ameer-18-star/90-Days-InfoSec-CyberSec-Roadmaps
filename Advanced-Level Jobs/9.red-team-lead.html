90-Day Roadmap For "Red Team Lead" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for offensive specialists ready to run campaign-length adversary simulations — designing the objective, commanding operator tradecraft and OPSEC discipline, and turning every operation into a genuine improvement in the organization's detection capability.

Phase 1: Operation Design & Threat-Informed Planning

Days 1-30: Designing a red team campaign around real adversary behavior, objectives, and the rules that keep it safe

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Threat-Informed Operations Campaign Planning Rules of Engagement Infrastructure Planning Objective-Based Scoping

Week 1: The Red Team Lead Role & Threat-Informed Operation Design

  • Study what changes between running a penetration test and leading a red team: the objective is testing detection and response, not maximizing findings
  • Learn how to select a relevant threat actor profile (via MITRE ATT&CK groups, CTI reporting) to emulate based on a client's actual industry risk
  • Study how operation objectives get defined collaboratively with the client: "reach the crown-jewel system" vs. "test our SOC's detection of X technique"
  • Learn how to translate a threat actor's known TTPs into a concrete operation plan mapped to MITRE ATT&CK techniques
  • Study how red team engagements differ in length and structure from standard pentests — weeks to months, not days
  • Review one real public red team or adversary emulation report and evaluate how its objective and threat profile were framed
  • Learn how to set success criteria that don't simply equal "we got domain admin" — detection time, response quality, and containment speed all matter
  • Document a full threat-informed operation plan for a fictional client, including selected actor profile and mapped objectives
Threat-Informed Ops MITRE ATT&CK Mapping Objective-Based Design Success Criteria

Task heading at the end of week: Threat-Informed Operation Plan

Build a complete operation plan for a fictional client mapped to one real threat actor's TTPs, including objectives, MITRE ATT&CK technique mapping, and success criteria beyond simple compromise.

Week 2: Rules of Engagement & Operational Safety at Campaign Scale

  • Study how Rules of Engagement (RoE) for a red team differ from a standard pentest: longer duration means more opportunity for unintended impact
  • Learn how to negotiate "white cell" awareness — who at the client knows the operation is happening, and how that's kept to a minimal trusted group
  • Study how to plan for and document emergency stop procedures specific to stealth operations (how do you halt without immediately revealing the operation?)
  • Learn how to scope authorized techniques carefully — physical access, social engineering, and destructive techniques each need explicit authorization
  • Practice writing a full RoE document for a fictional multi-week campaign, including white cell contacts and physical/social engineering boundaries
  • Study how to handle discovering a genuine, unrelated active compromise during a long-duration operation
  • Learn how data handling and operational security obligations differ when an operation spans weeks (where is captured data stored, how is it protected)
  • Document a campaign-scale pre-operation legal/safety checklist distinct from a short-engagement version
Campaign RoE White Cell Coordination Emergency Stop Procedures Social Engineering Authorization

Task heading at the end of week: Campaign Rules of Engagement

Write a complete Rules of Engagement document for a fictional multi-week campaign, including white cell coordination plan, authorized technique boundaries, and an emergency stop procedure designed for stealth operations.

Week 3: C2 Infrastructure Design & Operational Resilience

  • Study how red team C2 (command and control) infrastructure gets architected for resilience and attribution avoidance: redirectors, domain fronting concepts, categorized domains
  • Learn how to plan infrastructure that mimics legitimate traffic patterns to avoid trivial network-based detection
  • Practice setting up a basic redirector chain in a fully isolated lab using a framework like Sliver or Mythic with a legitimate-looking front domain
  • Study domain categorization and aging considerations — why a freshly registered domain is a red flag to most detection tooling
  • Learn how to plan infrastructure teardown and rotation procedures for if/when infrastructure gets burned mid-operation
  • Practice documenting an infrastructure architecture diagram for a fictional campaign, including redirectors, C2 profiles, and fallback channels
  • Study how infrastructure cost and complexity should scale with operation duration and the sophistication of the threat actor being emulated
  • Document a full C2 infrastructure plan and operational resilience procedure for your fictional campaign
C2 Infrastructure Redirectors Domain Categorization Sliver/Mythic

Task heading at the end of week: C2 Infrastructure Architecture

Build a full C2 infrastructure plan for a fictional campaign including redirector chain, domain strategy, and a fallback/teardown procedure, with one working lab redirector setup.

Week 4: Team Composition & Operator Direction

  • Study how a red team lead allocates operators across initial access, internal operations, and infrastructure maintenance roles
  • Learn how to structure operator handoffs across time zones or shifts for a campaign that needs to maintain access over weeks
  • Practice writing an operation runbook that lets an operator pick up an in-progress campaign thread without losing context
  • Study how a lead reviews operator actions for OPSEC discipline before they execute — catching a noisy technique before it burns the operation
  • Learn how to maintain a living operation log that captures every action taken, when, and why — both for OPSEC review and after-action reporting
  • Practice a mock daily operator sync reviewing 2-3 operators' planned next actions and approving or redirecting them
  • Study how to balance operator autonomy (senior operators need room to adapt in real time) against centralized OPSEC control
  • Document a reusable operation log template and an operator sync/review process for ongoing campaign management

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can design a threat-informed operation with real success criteria, write campaign-scale Rules of Engagement, architect resilient C2 infrastructure, and direct a team's operational discipline through a living operation log — the foundational toolkit of a Red Team Lead.

Phase 2: Advanced Tradecraft & Defender Collaboration

Days 31-60: Directing stealth tradecraft across the kill chain and turning operations into genuine purple team value

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Initial Access Tradecraft EDR Evasion Long-Dwell Operations Purple Team Collaboration Detection Validation

Week 5: Initial Access Tradecraft

  • Study how a lead directs initial access strategy: phishing, external exploitation, physical/USB drop, or vendor/supply-chain pretext — each with different risk and yield
  • Learn how to design a phishing pretext that's realistic enough to succeed without being unfairly deceptive in ways the client wouldn't authorize
  • Practice building one full phishing campaign plan (pretext, infrastructure, payload delivery) for a fictional operation, fully authorized in scope
  • Study how initial access payloads get built to minimize static and behavioral detection signatures
  • Learn how a lead decides which initial access vector best matches the threat actor being emulated, rather than just the easiest available option
  • Practice reviewing two operators' proposed initial access approaches and selecting/refining the one most aligned with operation objectives
  • Study how to handle an initial access attempt that fails or gets flagged early, and whether to retry, pivot vectors, or pause
  • Document an initial access decision framework mapping vector selection to threat actor profile and client risk tolerance

Build a full authorized phishing campaign plan (pretext, infrastructure, delivery) for a fictional operation, plus an initial access decision framework mapping vector choice to threat actor and risk tolerance.

Week 6: EDR/AV Evasion & Detection-Aware Tradecraft

  • Study how modern EDR detects behavior (process injection, suspicious API call chains, memory anomalies), not just file signatures
  • Learn living-off-the-land binary (LOLBin) usage as a way to blend malicious actions into expected system behavior
  • Practice testing a known LOLBin technique against a lab EDR/AV setup and documenting whether it's flagged
  • Study how a lead balances evasion sophistication against operational time budget — not every technique needs maximum stealth
  • Learn how to direct operators to vary technique signatures across an operation so the same detection rule doesn't catch every action
  • Practice reviewing a planned technique against your knowledge of common detection logic and flagging likely detection risk before execution
  • Study how to document evasion technique usage for the eventual after-action report, even when the goal was to remain undetected
  • Document a personal detection-risk assessment framework for evaluating planned techniques before operators execute them
EDR Behavioral Detection LOLBins Technique Variation Pre-Execution Risk Review

Task heading at the end of week: Detection-Risk Assessment Framework

Test one LOLBin technique against a lab EDR setup, document the result, and build a detection-risk assessment framework you'd use to review operator-planned techniques before execution.

Week 7: Long-Dwell Operations & Persistence Strategy

  • Study how genuine adversaries maintain access over weeks or months, and how a red team operation should realistically mirror that dwell time
  • Learn persistence mechanism selection: balancing reliability against detectability for an operation that needs to survive reboots and patches
  • Practice designing a persistence strategy for a fictional long-dwell operation, including a fallback mechanism if primary persistence is discovered
  • Study how a lead paces an operation deliberately — not rushing toward the objective, mirroring how patient real adversaries actually are
  • Learn how to direct "low and slow" reconnaissance and lateral movement that avoids triggering volume-based detection thresholds
  • Practice writing an operational pacing plan for a fictional 6-week campaign, spacing activity to avoid a burst pattern that's easy to detect
  • Study how a lead decides when dwell time has served its purpose and it's time to move toward the objective more directly
  • Document a long-dwell operational pacing and persistence strategy template for future campaigns
Persistence Strategy Operational Pacing Low-and-Slow Technique Dwell Time Management

Task heading at the end of week: Long-Dwell Pacing & Persistence Plan

Write a full operational pacing plan for a fictional 6-week campaign with a paired persistence strategy, including a fallback mechanism and the decision criteria for when to accelerate toward the objective.

Week 8: Purple Team Collaboration & Real-Time Defender Engagement

  • Study how purple teaming integrates the red team's operation with the defending blue team's learning in real time, rather than only at the final debrief
  • Learn how to structure a purple team checkpoint: pausing an operation at key moments to show defenders what happened and discuss detection gaps
  • Practice planning 2-3 purple team checkpoints within a fictional operation timeline, each tied to a specific completed technique
  • Study how to facilitate a purple team session without simply handing over the answer — guiding defenders toward their own detection insight
  • Learn how to balance "pure red team" value (testing detection blind) against "purple team" value (maximizing learning) within one engagement
  • Practice a mock purple team checkpoint session, walking a "defender" through one completed technique and co-developing a detection rule
  • Study how to document purple team session outcomes so they translate into actual production detection improvements afterward
  • Document a purple team checkpoint facilitation guide for integrating real-time defender collaboration into future campaigns
Purple Team Checkpoints Real-Time Collaboration Facilitated Detection Discovery Detection Improvement Translation

Task heading at the end of week: Purple Team Checkpoint Facilitation Guide

Plan 2-3 purple team checkpoints within a fictional operation timeline, run one mock checkpoint session with a peer, and write a facilitation guide for translating sessions into production detection improvements.

Phase 3: After-Action Reporting, Program Building & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Reports that drive real detection improvement, building a red team capability, and the certification/interview push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

After-Action Reporting Detection Gap Mapping Red Team Program Building Atomic/BAS Integration Interview Readiness

Week 9: After-Action Reporting & ATT&CK-Mapped Findings

  • Study how a red team after-action report differs from a pentest report: it tells the story of what the defenders saw and didn't see, technique by technique
  • Learn how to map every operation action to its corresponding MITRE ATT&CK technique for a structured, comparable report
  • Practice building a full ATT&CK technique timeline for one mock operation, marking each technique as detected/undetected/detected-but-not-actioned
  • Study how to write detection gap findings that are specific enough for a detection engineering team to act on immediately
  • Learn how to present the operation narrative compellingly — the same narrative-building skill from pentest leadership, applied to a longer story
  • Practice writing an executive summary for a campaign-length operation, emphasizing detection/response maturity rather than just compromise achieved
  • Study how to credit genuine defender wins in the report (techniques that WERE caught) to build trust and an accurate picture, not just list failures
  • Compile a full after-action report for one mock operation, including the ATT&CK technique timeline and balanced findings
After-Action Reports ATT&CK Technique Timeline Detection Gap Findings Balanced Reporting

Task heading at the end of week: Full After-Action Report

Build a complete after-action report for one mock operation, including an ATT&CK-mapped technique timeline marking detected/undetected actions and balanced findings crediting genuine defender wins.

Week 10: Translating Operations into Detection Engineering

  • Study how a red team lead's findings should feed directly into a detection engineering backlog, not just sit in a report
  • Learn how to write a detection engineering requirement from an operation finding: what data source, what logic, what expected behavior to alert on
  • Practice writing 3 detection requirements derived from your Week 9 after-action report's undetected techniques
  • Study how Atomic Red Team or breach-and-attack simulation (BAS) tooling lets defenders re-test a specific technique after building a new detection
  • Learn how a red team lead supports the validation loop — confirming a new detection actually fires against the technique that originally evaded it
  • Practice designing a re-test plan validating that 2 of your derived detection requirements, once implemented, would now catch the original technique
  • Study how this closed loop (operate, find gap, engineer detection, validate) is what separates a mature red team program from a one-off exercise
  • Document a full operation-to-detection-to-validation pipeline diagram for ongoing program use
Detection Requirements Atomic Red Team Breach-and-Attack Simulation Closed-Loop Validation

Task heading at the end of week: Operation-to-Detection Pipeline

Write 3 detection engineering requirements derived from your after-action report's gaps, design a re-test validation plan for 2 of them, and diagram the full closed-loop pipeline.

Week 11: Building a Red Team Capability

  • Study how a lead contributes to building (not just executing within) a red team program: cadence, scope evolution, tooling investment

  • Practice writing a 12-month red team program proposal for a fictional organization just establishing the capability
  • Study how to justify red team program investment to leadership when its "success" sometimes looks like finding nothing (strong detection)
  • Learn how internal red teams differ operationally and culturally from external consulting-style engagements
  • Practice writing a business case memo for in-housing a red team capability vs. continuing to contract it out
  • Study how to build and maintain operator skill development within a growing red team, including individual technique specialization
  • Compile your 90-day work into a single portfolio-style master red team leadership package

What Next

Where to take your red team career after Day 90

1. Specialize or Broaden

  • Decide: deepen toward elite tradecraft specialization, or broaden toward red team program management
  • Consider a Tradecraft Specialist track if evasion research and infrastructure design energized you most
  • Consider a Red Team Program Manager track if building and operating a capability stood out
  • Explore independent red team consulting once your reputation and operator network are strong enough
  • Look into specializing in cloud-native or OT/ICS red teaming as growth areas
  • Consider a high-stakes industry vertical specialization (critical infrastructure, finance, defense)
  • Pursue CRTL/OSEP if the elite tradecraft track is the clear direction
  • Pursue CISSP/PMP if the program management track is the clear direction
  • Join a specialized community for your chosen direction
  • Set a 6-12 month direction-setting goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Red Team Lead / Senior Red Team Operator / Adversary Emulation Lead roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description, emphasizing operation leadership and OPSEC discipline
  • Attend local BSides/DEF CON red team village meetups
  • Reach out to current red team leads and SpecterOps/TrustedSec-style practitioners on LinkedIn
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio updated with new operation artifacts and tradecraft research
  • Practice behavioral (STAR-format) mock interviews monthly
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider boutique red team consultancies for varied, high-leadership-exposure engagements
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly tradecraft research and new evasion technique disclosures
  • Do at least one new HackTheBox Pro Lab or red-team-style CTF per month to stay sharp
  • Re-test old evasion techniques against updated EDR products as detection evolves
  • Write a monthly technical blog post about something you learned
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio artifact every 90 days
  • Track new C2 framework and infrastructure tooling developments
  • Mentor junior testers moving toward red team readiness — teaching reinforces your own mastery
  • Read one new offensive security book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference talk per month
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT

  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (RoE, operation log, after-action template) for others to use
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on threat-informed operation design or purple teaming
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly

Stay disciplined, stay patient, and always close the loop back into detection.
