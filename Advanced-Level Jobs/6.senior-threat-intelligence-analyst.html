90-Day Roadmap For "Senior Threat Intelligence Analyst" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for analysts who already understand the intelligence cycle and are ready to own it — running collection against real requirements, applying structured analytical techniques, and producing finished intelligence that shapes real decisions.

Phase 1: Intelligence Requirements & Structured Collection

Days 1-30: Owning the intelligence cycle from requirements through structured, defensible collection

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

PIRs/Intelligence Requirements Collection Management OSINT Tradecraft Source Evaluation Dark Web Monitoring

Week 1: Senior CTI Role Calibration & Priority Intelligence Requirements

  • Study what changes between "consuming threat feeds" and "running an intelligence program": you now own what gets collected and why
  • Learn how Priority Intelligence Requirements (PIRs) get defined by working backward from real stakeholder decisions
  • Study the difference between strategic, operational, and tactical intelligence requirements, and who consumes each
  • Practice interviewing a fictional stakeholder (CISO, SOC manager, executive) to extract their actual intelligence needs, not assumed ones
  • Learn how to translate a vague stakeholder ask ("tell me about ransomware risk") into 3-5 specific, answerable PIRs
  • Study how PIRs should be revisited and refreshed as the threat landscape and business priorities shift
  • Review one real public CTI program description (vendor or enterprise writeup) and identify how its priorities appear structured
  • Document a full set of PIRs for a fictional organization across strategic, operational, and tactical levels
PIRs Stakeholder Interviews Strategic/Operational/Tactical Requirements Refresh

Task heading at the end of week: Priority Intelligence Requirements Set

Write a complete set of 5+ PIRs spanning strategic, operational, and tactical levels for a fictional organization, each traceable back to a specific stakeholder decision it would inform.

Week 2: Collection Management & Planning

  • Study how a collection plan maps each PIR to specific sources, methods, and collection cadence
  • Learn the major collection disciplines relevant to CTI: OSINT, technical (malware/network telemetry), HUMINT-adjacent (community/forum monitoring), and commercial feeds
  • Practice building a full collection plan mapping your Week 1 PIRs to specific named sources and collection methods
  • Study how collection gaps get identified and what to do when no good source exists for a stated requirement
  • Learn how to evaluate and onboard a new commercial threat intel feed against your actual PIRs rather than its marketing claims
  • Practice writing a feed evaluation memo comparing 2 fictional vendor offerings against specific PIRs
  • Study how collection plans get reviewed periodically for redundancy (paying for 3 feeds that report the same thing) and coverage gaps
  • Document a full collection management framework you could operate against on an ongoing basis
Collection Plans Collection Disciplines Feed Evaluation Coverage Gap Analysis

Task heading at the end of week: Collection Plan & Feed Evaluation

Build a full collection plan mapping your PIRs to named sources and methods, plus one feed evaluation memo comparing two fictional vendor offerings against specific requirements.

Week 3: Advanced OSINT Tradecraft

  • Study advanced OSINT methodology: pivoting from a single data point (domain, email, hash) into a fuller picture using structured technique, not random searching
  • Practice using passive DNS, WHOIS history, and certificate transparency logs to map infrastructure relationships
  • Learn how to use Shodan/Censys effectively for infrastructure attribution and exposure research
  • Study operational security for the researcher: sock puppet account hygiene, sandboxed browsing, avoiding tipping off a target that they're being researched
  • Practice a full OSINT pivot exercise starting from one public indicator and building out an infrastructure cluster
  • Learn how social media and forum monitoring tools support early warning of planned attacks or leaked data
  • Study legal and ethical boundaries of OSINT collection — what's fair game vs. what crosses into unauthorized access or harassment-adjacent behavior
  • Document a full OSINT pivot writeup demonstrating your research methodology, not just the final answer
Passive DNS/WHOIS Shodan/Censys Researcher OPSEC Infrastructure Pivoting

Task heading at the end of week: OSINT Infrastructure Pivot Writeup

Starting from one public indicator, build out a full infrastructure cluster using passive DNS, WHOIS, and certificate transparency, documenting your methodology step by step.

Week 4: Source Evaluation & Dark Web/Closed-Source Monitoring

  • Study the Admiralty Code (source reliability x information credibility) for rating raw intelligence systematically
  • Learn how to apply consistent source evaluation across open, closed, and commercial sources so analysis isn't built on unvetted claims
  • Practice rating 5 sample pieces of raw intelligence using the Admiralty Code and justifying each rating
  • Study dark web/closed-forum monitoring at a conceptual and safety level: access methods, legal considerations, and what's realistically achievable without specialized access
  • Learn how ransomware leak site monitoring provides early visibility into victim organizations and group activity patterns
  • Practice (using safe, legal, publicly documented examples) tracing how a ransomware leak site posting gets identified and processed into intelligence
  • Study how to avoid single-source reliance, especially for high-confidence claims that will inform major decisions
  • Document a source evaluation framework and rating log you'll apply to all incoming raw intelligence going forward
Admiralty Code Source Reliability Ransomware Leak Sites Multi-Source Corroboration

Task heading at the end of week: Source Evaluation Framework

Build a complete source evaluation framework using the Admiralty Code, rate 5 sample pieces of raw intelligence with it, and document your rationale for each rating.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can define PIRs traceable to real stakeholder decisions, build a structured collection plan against them, conduct disciplined OSINT pivoting, and rate source reliability consistently — the foundational toolkit of a Senior Threat Intelligence Analyst.

Phase 2: Structured Analysis & Adversary Tracking

Days 31-60: Applying rigorous analytical technique and building durable, evolving threat actor knowledge

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Structured Analytic Techniques Threat Actor Tracking Campaign Analysis Geopolitical Context Confidence Language

Week 5: Structured Analytic Techniques (SATs)

  • Study why structured analytic techniques exist: combating cognitive bias and groupthink in intelligence analysis
  • Learn Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) as a method for systematically testing multiple explanations against evidence
  • Practice running a full ACH matrix for one ambiguous incident scenario with at least 3 competing hypotheses
  • Study Key Assumptions Check and how to surface unstated assumptions baked into an analytic judgment
  • Learn devil's advocacy and "what if" analysis as techniques for pressure-testing a leading hypothesis before publishing
  • Practice applying a Key Assumptions Check to one of your own Phase 1 analytic products, surfacing assumptions you hadn't stated
  • Study how SATs get applied efficiently under real time pressure rather than as a slow academic exercise
  • Document a personal SAT toolkit with quick-reference guides for when to use each technique
Analysis of Competing Hypotheses Key Assumptions Check Devil's Advocacy Cognitive Bias Mitigation

Task heading at the end of week: ACH Matrix & Assumptions Check

Run a full Analysis of Competing Hypotheses matrix for one ambiguous scenario, then apply a Key Assumptions Check to your leading hypothesis before finalizing a judgment.

Week 6: Threat Actor Tracking & Profile Maintenance

  • Study how a senior analyst builds and maintains living threat actor profiles, not one-off reports that go stale immediately
  • Learn how to structure a threat actor profile: aliases, motivation, sponsorship assessment, TTPs, targeting patterns, known tooling, confidence level per claim
  • Practice building one complete, properly-sourced threat actor profile for a real, publicly-documented group
  • Study how to track a group's evolution over time — tooling changes, target shifts, apparent rebranding or splintering
  • Learn how diamond model elements (adversary, infrastructure, capability, victim) keep a profile organized and complete
  • Practice writing an update to an existing profile reflecting new reporting, distinguishing what's confirmed vs. assessed
  • Study how internal "named" threat actor tracking differs from relying purely on vendor-assigned names, and the tradeoffs of each
  • Document a reusable threat actor profile template you'll apply to all future tracked groups
Threat Actor Profiles Diamond Model Profile Maintenance Naming Conventions

Task heading at the end of week: Living Threat Actor Profile

Build one complete, properly-sourced threat actor profile for a real group using your template, then write one update reflecting hypothetical new reporting.

Week 7: Campaign Analysis & Pattern Recognition

  • Study how individual incidents get connected into a recognized "campaign" through shared infrastructure, tooling, or targeting
  • Learn how to build a campaign timeline correlating multiple separate incidents/reports into one coherent narrative
  • Practice clustering a set of mock incidents using shared indicators and TTPs to identify whether they form one campaign
  • Study how targeting pattern analysis (which sectors, regions, or company sizes get hit) reveals likely motivation and future risk
  • Learn how to distinguish a genuine campaign from coincidental similarity (commodity malware used by many unrelated actors)
  • Practice writing a campaign analysis report connecting your clustered mock incidents into one narrative with confidence-rated conclusions
  • Study how campaign tracking feeds proactive recommendations (likely next targets, recommended detections) rather than just historical description
  • Document a campaign clustering methodology you can reapply to future incident sets
Campaign Clustering Targeting Pattern Analysis Timeline Correlation Proactive Recommendations

Task heading at the end of week: Campaign Analysis Report

Cluster a set of mock incidents into a coherent campaign narrative using shared indicators and targeting patterns, and write a confidence-rated campaign analysis report with forward-looking recommendations.

Week 8: Geopolitical Context & Confidence Language Discipline

  • Study how geopolitical events (sanctions, conflicts, elections) correlate with shifts in nation-state and hacktivist cyber activity
  • Learn how to incorporate geopolitical context into a threat assessment without overreaching into pure political punditry
  • Practice writing a short geopolitical context section for one threat assessment, grounded in cyber-relevant implications only
  • Study standardized confidence language (low/moderate/high confidence) and words-of-estimative-probability conventions used in professional intelligence writing
  • Learn why precise, consistent confidence language matters more than how it might sound — vague hedging undermines trust over time
  • Practice rewriting a poorly-hedged mock analytic judgment into properly calibrated confidence language
  • Study how to communicate a change in confidence level over time as new evidence arrives, without appearing to have been simply wrong before
  • Document a personal confidence-language style guide for consistent use across all your future products
Geopolitical Context Words of Estimative Probability Confidence Calibration Analytic Rigor

Task heading at the end of week: Confidence-Calibrated Threat Assessment

Write one threat assessment incorporating a grounded geopolitical context section, using your confidence-language style guide consistently throughout the analytic judgments.

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You can apply structured analytic techniques to pressure-test judgments, maintain living threat actor profiles, cluster incidents into campaign-level analysis, and write with disciplined, calibrated confidence language — the analytical rigor that defines senior CTI work.

Phase 3: Finished Intelligence Production, Dissemination & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Writing intelligence that drives decisions, building a program others trust, and the certification/interview push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Finished Intelligence Writing Dissemination Strategy CTI-to-Detection Bridge Program Metrics Interview Readiness

Week 9: Finished Intelligence Product Writing

  • Study the standard finished intelligence product types: spot reports, intelligence summaries, deep-dive assessments, executive briefs
  • Learn the BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front) writing convention and why intelligence writing leads with the conclusion, not the buildup
  • Practice writing one spot report (rapid, short) and one deep-dive assessment (comprehensive) on related but different aspects of one topic
  • Study how to tailor the same underlying analysis for different audiences: technical SOC consumers vs. executive leadership
  • Learn how visual products (infographics, heat maps, timelines) complement written analysis for faster consumption
  • Practice building one visual summary product distilling a complex assessment into a single page
  • Study how to write actionable recommendations that are specific enough to act on, not generic "increase monitoring" advice
  • Compile a finished intelligence portfolio piece combining a deep-dive assessment, an executive brief version, and a visual summary
BLUF Writing Spot Reports Audience Tailoring Visual Products

Task heading at the end of week: Multi-Format Finished Intelligence Set

Write one deep-dive assessment, then produce an executive brief version and a one-page visual summary of the same analysis for different audiences.

Week 10: The CTI-to-Action Bridge

  • Study why intelligence that doesn't change a decision or detection capability has limited operational value, however accurate
  • Learn how to translate a finished assessment into concrete detection requirements for a SOC/detection engineering team
  • Practice writing a "so what does this mean for us" section bridging your Phase 2 campaign analysis into specific defensive recommendations
  • Study how CTI feeds into vulnerability prioritization (which CVEs matter because actors are actively exploiting them)
  • Learn how to track whether your intelligence actually got acted upon — closing the loop on impact, not just publishing and moving on
  • Practice designing a simple feedback mechanism for stakeholders to confirm whether a product was useful and acted upon
  • Study how proactive warning products (early indicators of likely future targeting) differ from retrospective campaign reports
  • Document a CTI-to-action framework mapping your product types to their intended downstream action
Detection Requirements Vulnerability Prioritization Impact Feedback Loops Proactive Warning

Task heading at the end of week: CTI-to-Action Bridge Document

Write a "so what" section bridging your campaign analysis into specific detection requirements, plus a simple feedback mechanism design for tracking whether stakeholders acted on it.

Week 11: Program Operations, Metrics & External Engagement

  • Study how a CTI program demonstrates its value to leadership: not raw report counts, but decisions influenced and risks mitigated
  • Learn how to define meaningful CTI program metrics (PIR coverage %, time-to-publish, stakeholder feedback scores)
  • Practice building a simple CTI program dashboard summarizing these metrics for a fictional quarter
  • Study how information-sharing communities (ISACs, trusted peer groups) operate and what value participation provides both ways
  • Learn how to responsibly share intelligence externally (sanitization, TLP markings) without exposing sensitive sourcing
  • Practice applying Traffic Light Protocol (TLP) markings correctly to a set of mock intelligence products for different sharing contexts
  • Study how a senior analyst contributes to industry-wide knowledge (conference talks, published research) while protecting client confidentiality
  • Compile your 90-day work into a single portfolio-style master CTI program report
Program Metrics ISAC Participation TLP Markings Responsible Sharing

Task heading at the end of week: CTI Program Dashboard & TLP Practice

Build a simple CTI program metrics dashboard for a fictional quarter, and apply correct TLP markings to 3 mock products intended for different sharing audiences.

Week 12: Certification & Interview Readiness

  • Review and attempt practice questions aligned to a target cert (e.g. GIAC GCTI, CTIA, or CISSP)
  • Do a full mock rapid-response exercise: a breaking news event drops, produce a spot report and stakeholder notification within 2 hours
  • Prepare answers for senior CTI interview questions (walk through building a threat actor profile, explain a confidence-language judgment call, describe influencing a real decision)
  • Build a one-page resume bullet list translating your 90 days into measurable analytical and program-impact achievements
  • Polish your portfolio: pin your best 3 artifacts (PIR-to-collection pipeline, threat actor profile, multi-format finished intelligence set)
  • Practice a mock behavioral interview using STAR format for your campaign analysis and stakeholder-influence stories
  • Research target organizations/vendors and tailor your resume + LinkedIn headline to emphasize finished-intelligence production specifically
  • Plan your next 90 days: pick a direction (strategic/geopolitical CTI specialist, CTI program lead, or technical CTI/malware-focused depth)
Mock Interviews STAR Behavioral Prep Resume Building Cert Prep

Task heading at the end of week: Job-Ready Senior CTI Portfolio

Finalize a portfolio (PIR-to-collection pipeline, threat actor profile, finished intelligence set), resume bullets, and 2 polished STAR-format interview stories for Senior Threat Intelligence Analyst roles.

Congratulations! You now have a complete strategic CTI toolkit (requirements management, structured collection, rigorous analytic technique, threat actor/campaign tracking, finished intelligence production, program operations) plus a portfolio of real artifacts and a job-ready application package — the foundation of a genuinely senior Threat Intelligence Analyst.

What Next

Where to take your threat intelligence career after Day 90

1. Specialize or Broaden

  • Decide: deepen toward strategic/geopolitical CTI, technical CTI (malware-adjacent), or broaden toward CTI program leadership
  • Consider a Strategic Intelligence specialization if geopolitical context and executive products energized you most
  • Consider a Technical CTI track (closer to SOC Tier 3/malware analysis) if indicator-level depth appeals more
  • Explore CTI Program Lead/Manager as a path if requirements management and metrics stood out
  • Look into vendor-side CTI research roles (Mandiant, Recorded Future, CrowdStrike) for industry-wide visibility
  • Consider specializing in a high-stakes industry vertical (financial sector fraud intel, critical infrastructure)
  • Pursue GCTI/CTIA if the technical/structured-analysis track is the clear direction
  • Pursue program management or CISSP-adjacent paths if leadership is the clear direction
  • Join a specialized community for your chosen direction
  • Set a 6-12 month direction-setting goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Senior Threat Intelligence Analyst / CTI Lead / Threat Researcher roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description, emphasizing finished-product writing and analytical rigor
  • Attend local CTI/OSINT meetups and FIRST.org events
  • Reach out to senior CTI analysts and researchers on LinkedIn for informational chats
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio updated with new threat actor profiles and analytic writeups
  • Practice behavioral (STAR-format) mock interviews monthly
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider vendor CTI research teams for high-volume exposure to diverse threats
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly threat research and geopolitical developments relevant to cyber
  • Update your tracked threat actor profiles regularly as new reporting emerges
  • Re-apply SATs to old judgments periodically to check your calibration over time
  • Write a monthly post or analytic piece about something you've researched
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio artifact every 90 days
  • Track new OSINT tooling and CTI platform developments
  • Mentor junior analysts — teaching reinforces your own analytical discipline
  • Read one new intelligence analysis or geopolitics book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference talk per month
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create an "introduction to real CTI work" series for analysts who've only seen finished reports
  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (threat actor profile, PIR framework) for others to use
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on structured analytic techniques or OSINT methodology
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly
  • Pair up with another aspiring senior analyst for accountability
  • Contribute documentation or templates to a community resource hub
  • Review and give feedback on a peer's threat actor profile or analytic product
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone starting out

Remember: Threat intelligence that doesn't change a decision is just trivia with good production values. Every PIR, profile, and finished product you write exists to answer one question: "does the person reading this now know what to do differently?" Stay rigorous, stay honest about your confidence level, and always trace your work back to the decision it was meant to inform.
