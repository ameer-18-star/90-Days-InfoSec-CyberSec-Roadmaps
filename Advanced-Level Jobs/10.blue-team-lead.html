90-Day Roadmap For "Blue Team Lead" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for defenders ready to own the whole defensive operation — directing a SOC team, owning detection engineering as a program rather than a task list, and turning every red team finding and real incident into measurable, lasting improvement.

Phase 1: Defensive Architecture & Detection Program Ownership

Days 1-30: Owning the SOC's detection capability as a system, not a collection of individual rules

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

Detection Engineering Program SOC Architecture Visibility Coverage ATT&CK Defense Mapping Team Direction

Week 1: The Blue Team Lead Role & Defensive Posture Ownership

  • Study what changes between being a strong SOC analyst and leading the SOC: you now own whether the team's collective output is improving over time
  • Learn how a blue team lead is accountable for outcomes (mean time to detect, coverage gaps closed) rather than individual alert resolution
  • Study how to inherit and audit an existing detection program: what's actually being monitored vs. what's assumed to be monitored
  • Practice reviewing a fictional SOC's current rule set and identifying which rules are stale, duplicative, or no longer tied to real risk
  • Learn how to build an honest baseline assessment of current defensive maturity before proposing any improvements
  • Study how a lead balances "keep the lights on" alert triage capacity against time invested in program improvement
  • Review one real public SOC/detection program writeup and evaluate how its maturity and priorities appear structured
  • Document a baseline defensive posture assessment for a fictional organization's SOC, including a stale-rule audit

Task heading at the end of week: Defensive Posture Baseline Assessment

Audit a fictional SOC's existing rule set for staleness and duplication, and write a complete baseline defensive posture assessment documenting current maturity honestly before proposing changes.

Week 2: Visibility & Telemetry Coverage Mapping

  • Study how "detection coverage" is meaningless without first knowing what telemetry actually exists to detect against
  • Learn how to map data source coverage (endpoint, network, identity, cloud, application logs) against what's actually flowing into the SIEM
  • Practice building a telemetry coverage map for a fictional environment, flagging which asset classes have strong, partial, or no logging
  • Study how log retention, parsing quality, and field normalization affect whether collected data is actually usable for detection
  • Learn how to prioritize closing visibility gaps based on risk (a blind spot on the internet-facing perimeter matters more than one on an air-gapped test server)
  • Practice writing a visibility gap remediation plan prioritizing 5 identified blind spots by risk and effort
  • Study how EDR, network sensors, and cloud-native logging each contribute differently to overall visibility, and where overlaps and gaps commonly occur
  • Document a full telemetry coverage map and prioritized remediation plan for a fictional environment
Telemetry Coverage Log Source Mapping Visibility Gap Prioritization Field Normalization

Task heading at the end of week: Telemetry Coverage Map & Remediation Plan

Build a full telemetry coverage map for a fictional environment identifying logging blind spots, and write a prioritized remediation plan for closing the top 5 gaps by risk.

Week 3: ATT&CK-Mapped Defensive Coverage Strategy

  • Study how to use the MITRE ATT&CK framework as a defensive planning tool, not just an offensive reference
  • Learn how to build a defensive ATT&CK Navigator heatmap reflecting actual detection confidence per technique, not aspirational coverage
  • Practice mapping a fictional SOC's existing detection rules to specific ATT&CK techniques and identifying coverage density by tactic
  • Study how to prioritize which uncovered techniques to address first based on relevance to your organization's actual threat profile
  • Learn how detection coverage should weight toward techniques adversaries relevant to your industry actually use, not generic completeness
  • Practice writing a detection roadmap prioritizing 8 uncovered or weakly-covered techniques based on threat relevance
  • Study how ATT&CK Defense (D3FEND) complements the offensive framework by cataloging specific defensive countermeasures per technique
  • Document a full ATT&CK-mapped defensive coverage heatmap and an 8-technique prioritized improvement roadmap
ATT&CK Navigator Defensive Heatmapping D3FEND Threat-Relevant Prioritization

Task heading at the end of week: ATT&CK Defensive Coverage Heatmap

Build a defensive ATT&CK Navigator heatmap mapping existing rules to techniques, and write a prioritized 8-technique improvement roadmap weighted toward your organization's actual threat relevance.

Week 4: SOC Team Structure & Daily Operations Direction

  • Study how a lead structures a SOC across shifts/tiers and balances alert triage capacity against detection engineering and hunting time
  • Learn how to design an escalation path that gets the right alert to the right analyst quickly without overwhelming senior staff with noise
  • Practice writing a tiered escalation matrix for a fictional SOC, defining what triggers Tier 1 to Tier 2 to lead-level escalation
  • Study how to run effective shift handoffs so context doesn't get lost between analysts working different hours
  • Learn how to balance individual analyst development time (learning, certifications) against operational coverage needs
  • Practice designing a weekly team schedule balancing triage coverage, detection engineering sprints, and dedicated learning time
  • Study how a lead reviews analyst alert-handling quality without becoming a bottleneck reviewing every single ticket
  • Document a full SOC team structure, escalation matrix, and weekly operating rhythm for a fictional team of 8 analysts

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can audit an inherited detection program honestly, map telemetry and ATT&CK coverage to find real gaps, and structure a SOC team's operating rhythm to balance triage against improvement work — the foundational toolkit of a Blue Team Lead.

Phase 2: Detection Engineering Leadership & Adversary Response

Days 31-60: Running detection engineering as a structured program and directing the team's response to real and simulated attacks

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Detection-as-Code Program Major Incident Direction Red Team Collaboration Threat Hunting Program Tool/Platform Strategy

Week 5: Detection Engineering as a Structured Program

  • Study how a mature detection engineering function operates: intake, development, testing, deployment, and tuning as a repeatable pipeline
  • Learn how to set up a detection-as-code workflow with version control, peer review, and automated testing before rules reach production
  • Practice designing a detection rule lifecycle process for a fictional team, including who approves a rule before it goes live
  • Study how to track and reduce false-positive rates systematically rather than tuning reactively rule by rule
  • Learn how to prioritize a detection engineering backlog using the same risk-based thinking from your Phase 1 coverage roadmap
  • Practice writing a detection engineering sprint plan for a fictional 2-week cycle, with 5 prioritized rules to build and test
  • Study how to measure detection engineering output meaningfully (rules shipped, false-positive rate trend, coverage % growth)
  • Document a full detection engineering program process and a sample sprint plan ready for team adoption

Week 6: Major Incident Direction from the Defensive Side

  • Study how a blue team lead directs a SOC's response during a major incident: coordinating analysts, escalating appropriately, and maintaining situational awareness
  • Learn how to make real-time containment recommendations based on incomplete information without panicking the team or the business
  • Practice leading a mock major incident drill, directing 2-3 simulated analysts through investigation and containment decisions
  • Study how a lead balances "stop the bleeding now" against "preserve evidence for proper investigation" during active response
  • Learn how to keep the SOC operating normally for unrelated alerts while a subset of the team is consumed by a major incident
  • Practice writing a surge staffing plan for how your SOC would handle a major incident without dropping baseline coverage
  • Study how post-incident, the lead ensures findings get converted into new detections rather than just closing the ticket
  • Document a major incident direction playbook covering team coordination, surge staffing, and the detection-conversion follow-through
Incident Direction Surge Staffing Containment Under Uncertainty Post-Incident Detection Conversion

Task heading at the end of week: Major Incident Direction Playbook

Week 7: Red Team & Purple Team Collaboration from the Blue Side

  • Study how a blue team lead prepares the SOC to genuinely benefit from a red team engagement rather than treating it as an adversarial test to "win"
  • Learn how to participate constructively in purple team checkpoints — asking the right questions to extract maximum detection insight
  • Practice a mock purple team checkpoint from the defender's side, working through one red team technique and identifying why it wasn't detected
  • Study how to triage a red team after-action report into a prioritized detection engineering backlog rather than letting findings sit unaddressed
  • Learn how to communicate red team findings to the broader SOC team constructively, avoiding blame while building urgency
  • Practice writing a post-engagement detection improvement plan derived from a mock red team after-action report
  • Study how to use breach-and-attack simulation (BAS) or Atomic Red Team for continuous validation between formal red team engagements
  • Document a red team collaboration protocol covering pre-engagement prep, real-time checkpoint participation, and post-engagement follow-through
Purple Team Participation Finding Triage Constructive Team Communication Continuous Validation (BAS)

Task heading at the end of week: Red Team Collaboration Protocol

Week 8: Proactive Threat Hunting Program Direction

  • Study how a lead builds and sustains a hunting program rather than letting hunting happen only when an analyst has spare time
  • Learn how to source hunt hypotheses systematically: from CTI, from ATT&CK coverage gaps, and from prior incident patterns
  • Practice designing a recurring hunting cadence (e.g. one structured hunt per sprint) with hypothesis assignment and reporting requirements
  • Study how to track hunting program value: hunts run, true positives found, and conversion rate into permanent detections
  • Learn how a lead reviews a hunt's methodology for rigor before its conclusions get trusted, similar to peer-reviewing detection rules
  • Practice reviewing a mock hunt report for methodology gaps and providing structured feedback to strengthen it
  • Study how hunting program maturity progresses from ad hoc to fully structured, hypothesis-driven, and metric-tracked
  • Document a full threat hunting program design including cadence, hypothesis sourcing, and value-tracking metrics

Phase 3: Program Metrics, Tooling Strategy & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Proving the program's value, choosing the right tools, and the certification/interview push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Defensive Program Metrics SIEM/EDR Tooling Strategy Stakeholder Reporting Team Development Interview Readiness

Week 9: Defensive Program Metrics That Matter

  • Study why mean time to detect (MTTD) and mean time to respond (MTTR) matter more to leadership than raw alert volume
  • Learn how to define a balanced metrics set: detection coverage growth, false-positive trend, hunt-to-detection conversion, MTTD/MTTR
  • Practice building a defensive program metrics dashboard for a fictional quarter combining detection engineering, hunting, and incident response data
  • Study how to avoid vanity metrics that look good but don't reflect real defensive improvement (e.g. raw alert count as a "productivity" measure)
  • Learn how to present a metrics trend honestly even when it's not improving, and frame the corrective plan alongside it
  • Practice writing a monthly defensive operations report combining metrics, narrative context, and next-period priorities
  • Study how metrics should evolve as program maturity increases — early-stage programs track different things than mature ones
  • Document a full defensive program metrics framework ready for ongoing operation

What Next

Where to take your blue team career after Day 90

1. Specialize or Broaden

  • Decide: deepen toward detection engineering specialization, or broaden toward SOC manager/director leadership
  • Consider a Detection Engineering Lead track if pipeline design and ATT&CK coverage work energized you most
  • Consider a SOC Manager/Director track if team development and program metrics stood out
  • Explore a Purple Team Lead hybrid path if collaboration with offensive teams was your strongest area
  • Look into Detection-as-a-Service or MSSP leadership roles for cross-client program exposure
  • Consider specializing in a high-stakes industry vertical (critical infrastructure, finance, healthcare)
  • Pursue GDSA/GCDA if the technical detection engineering track is the clear direction
  • Pursue CISSP/PMP if the broader SOC leadership track is the clear direction
  • Join a specialized community for your chosen direction
  • Set a 6-12 month direction-setting goal

2. Apply & Network

  • Apply to Blue Team Lead / SOC Lead / Detection Engineering Lead roles weekly
  • Tailor your resume per job description, emphasizing program ownership and measurable improvement
  • Attend local BSides/DEF CON blue team village meetups
  • Reach out to current SOC/blue team leads on LinkedIn for informational chats
  • Ask for referrals once you've built real community connections
  • Keep your portfolio updated with new program and pipeline artifacts
  • Practice behavioral (STAR-format) mock interviews monthly
  • Track applications and follow up professionally
  • Consider MSSP or consulting-adjacent blue team roles for varied program exposure
  • Set a weekly application/networking quota and stick to it

3. Keep Building

  • Stay current with weekly detection engineering and threat research content
  • Re-run your ATT&CK coverage heatmap quarterly as a personal practice habit
  • Write a monthly post about a program improvement or leadership judgment call you navigated
  • Build a second, more advanced portfolio artifact every 90 days
  • Track new SIEM/EDR platform capabilities and detection engineering tooling
  • Mentor junior analysts moving toward lead readiness — teaching reinforces your own mastery
  • Read one new blue team/leadership book per month
  • Attend at least one virtual conference talk per month
  • Seek structured feedback on your team direction and stakeholder communication skills
  • Reassess your roadmap every quarter and adjust goals

4. Give Back

  • Turn your 90-day notes into content for Virtual Mentors SOIT
  • Create a "becoming a blue team lead" series for senior analysts aspiring to lead a SOC
  • Answer questions in community forums/Discords regularly
  • Open-source one of your templates (coverage map, sprint plan, escalation matrix) for others to use
  • Host a free workshop/webinar on detection engineering program design or SOC leadership
  • Write a "lessons learned" retrospective on your 90 days publicly
  • Pair up with another aspiring lead for accountability
  • Contribute documentation fixes to a detection tool you use often
  • Review and give feedback on a peer's detection rule or coverage roadmap
  • Pay it forward — share this exact roadmap with someone starting out
