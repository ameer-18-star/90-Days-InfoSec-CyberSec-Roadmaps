90-Day Roadmap For "SOC Analyst – Tier 3" Mastery

An advanced-track plan for analysts who've already worked Tier 1/2 alert triage and are ready to operate as the SOC's escalation point — threat hunting, deep malware/forensic analysis, detection engineering, and leading incident response when things get serious.

Phase 1: Advanced Detection Engineering & Threat Intelligence

Days 1-30: SIEM/detection-as-code, MITRE ATT&CK mastery, and operationalized threat intelligence

🎯 Phase 1 Goals

MITRE ATT&CK Detection Engineering Threat Intelligence (CTI) SIEM Architecture Sigma Rules

Week 1: Tier 3 Role Calibration & MITRE ATT&CK Mastery

  • Map the Tier 1/2/3 escalation model: what specifically gets pushed up to Tier 3 and why
  • Study MITRE ATT&CK at depth: tactics, techniques, sub-techniques, and data sources per technique
  • Learn how to build an ATT&CK Navigator heatmap reflecting your organization's actual visibility gaps
  • Study the Pyramid of Pain and why IOC-only detection is the weakest layer to rely on
  • Learn the Diamond Model of Intrusion Analysis as a complement to the ATT&CK kill chain view
  • Review 3 real APT group profiles (MITRE ATT&CK groups pages) and their characteristic TTPs
  • Study how detection coverage maturity gets measured against ATT&CK (technique coverage %)
  • Document your own ATT&CK Navigator heatmap for a fictional org's current detection gaps
MITRE ATT&CK Navigator Pyramid of Pain Diamond Model APT Profiling

Task heading at the end of week: ATT&CK Coverage Heatmap

Build a full ATT&CK Navigator heatmap for a fictional org, color-coded by detection confidence (none/partial/strong), and identify your top 5 coverage gaps.

Week 2: SIEM Architecture & Detection-as-Code

  • Study SIEM data pipeline architecture: log sources, parsers, normalization, indexing, retention tiers
  • Set up a home/lab SIEM (Splunk Free, Elastic Security, or Wazuh) and ingest sample log sources
  • Learn Sigma rule format as a vendor-agnostic detection-as-code standard
  • Practice writing 5 Sigma rules covering common ATT&CK techniques, then convert them to your SIEM's query language
  • Study detection rule lifecycle: draft, test against known-good/known-bad data, tune, deploy, monitor for drift
  • Learn how false-positive rate and detection logic both get version-controlled like code (detection-as-code in Git)
  • Practice setting up a basic CI check that validates Sigma rule syntax before merge
  • Document a detection engineering workflow diagram from idea to production deployment
Sigma Rules Splunk/Elastic/Wazuh Detection-as-Code Rule Lifecycle

Task heading at the end of week: Sigma Rule Pack

Write 5 Sigma rules covering distinct ATT&CK techniques, convert each to your lab SIEM's native query language, and test against sample log data.

Week 3: Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) Operationalization

  • Study the intelligence cycle: direction, collection, processing, analysis, dissemination, feedback
  • Learn strategic vs. operational vs. tactical threat intelligence and who consumes each level
  • Practice using MISP (or a similar free CTI platform) to manage and correlate indicators
  • Study STIX/TAXII as the standard format/protocol for sharing structured threat intelligence
  • Learn how to pivot from a single IOC to a full campaign profile using OSINT and CTI platforms
  • Practice writing a finished intelligence product: a threat actor profile brief for a fictional relevant adversary
  • Study how CTI directly drives detection engineering priorities (closing the loop from Week 2)
  • Learn how to assess CTI source reliability and avoid acting on low-confidence reporting
MISP STIX/TAXII Intelligence Cycle OSINT Pivoting

Task heading at the end of week: Threat Actor Intelligence Brief

Write a complete threat actor profile brief (TTPs, targeting, relevant IOCs) for one real, publicly-documented adversary group relevant to your industry context.

Week 4: Network Forensics & Deep Packet Analysis

  • Study advanced Wireshark/tshark filtering for command-and-control (C2) traffic identification
  • Learn to recognize C2 beaconing patterns in traffic: regular intervals, jitter, encoded payloads
  • Practice analyzing a malicious PCAP (e.g. from Malware-Traffic-Analysis.net) end-to-end
  • Study DNS tunneling and exfiltration-over-DNS detection patterns
  • Learn TLS/JA3 fingerprinting basics for identifying malicious client behavior even in encrypted traffic
  • Practice extracting files/payloads from PCAP captures using NetworkMiner or Wireshark's export feature
  • Study how Zeek (formerly Bro) logs provide structured network metadata for faster triage than raw PCAP
  • Write a full network forensics report for one analyzed malicious PCAP sample
Wireshark/tshark Zeek JA3 Fingerprinting C2 Beaconing

Task heading at the end of week: PCAP Forensics Report

Fully analyze one malicious PCAP sample, extract any payloads, identify C2 indicators, and write a forensic report suitable for an incident timeline.

🎯 Milestone 1 Achievement

You can map detection coverage against MITRE ATT&CK, write and deploy detection-as-code rules, operationalize threat intelligence into a finished product, and perform deep network forensic analysis — the foundational toolkit of a Tier 3 SOC Analyst.

Phase 2: Threat Hunting & Malware Analysis

Days 31-60: Proactive hypothesis-driven hunting and hands-on static/dynamic malware analysis

🎯 Phase 2 Goals

Threat Hunting Static Malware Analysis Dynamic Analysis/Sandboxing Memory Forensics EDR Internals

Week 5: Hypothesis-Driven Threat Hunting

  • Study the difference between alert-driven (reactive) work and hypothesis-driven (proactive) hunting
  • Learn the threat hunting loop: hypothesis, investigation, uncover new patterns, inform automated detection
  • Study how to form a good hunting hypothesis from CTI, ATT&CK gaps, or anomaly observations
  • Practice running a structured hunt for one specific technique (e.g. living-off-the-land binary abuse) across lab log data
  • Learn baseline-and-deviation hunting: establishing "normal" before you can spot "abnormal"
  • Study how successful hunts get converted into permanent detection rules (closing the loop again)
  • Practice documenting a hunt using a structured hunt report template (hypothesis, scope, findings, follow-up)
  • Learn how hunting metrics (hunts run, true positives found, detections created) demonstrate program value
Threat Hunting Loop LOLBins Baseline & Deviation Hunt Reporting

Task heading at the end of week: Structured Hunt Report

Run a full hypothesis-driven hunt for one ATT&CK technique against lab data, and document it in a structured hunt report including any new detection rule created.

Week 6: Static Malware Analysis

  • Set up an isolated malware analysis VM (FLARE VM or a hardened, network-isolated lab) — safety first
  • Study PE file format basics: headers, sections, imports/exports, and what they reveal pre-execution
  • Practice using PEStudio/PEiD to triage a sample's suspicious indicators before deeper analysis
  • Learn string extraction and entropy analysis to spot packing/obfuscation in a sample
  • Study YARA rule writing for identifying malware families based on static characteristics
  • Practice writing 3 YARA rules against known malware samples (from a safe, curated repository)
  • Learn basic disassembly reading in Ghidra: identifying suspicious API calls and control flow
  • Document a static analysis report for one sample, including a YARA rule you wrote for it
FLARE VM PEStudio YARA Ghidra

Task heading at the end of week: Static Analysis + YARA Rule

Perform a full static analysis on one malware sample in an isolated lab, write a YARA rule that detects it, and validate the rule against a clean control sample.

Week 7: Dynamic Analysis & Sandboxing

  • Study dynamic analysis principles: controlled detonation, behavioral observation, network/file system monitoring
  • Practice using a sandbox (Any.Run, Joe Sandbox Cloud free tier, or a self-hosted Cuckoo/CAPE instance) safely
  • Learn Procmon and Process Hacker/System Informer for live behavioral observation in an isolated VM
  • Study common malware behaviors: persistence mechanisms, process injection, anti-analysis/anti-VM techniques
  • Practice detonating one sample and documenting its full behavioral chain (process tree, file/registry changes, network calls)
  • Learn how dynamic analysis findings feed directly back into Sigma/EDR detection rule creation
  • Study how to recognize sandbox-evasion techniques malware uses to detect it's being analyzed
  • Write a combined static+dynamic malware analysis report ready for sharing with the broader SOC
Any.Run/CAPE Procmon Process Injection Anti-VM Detection

Task heading at the end of week: Combined Malware Analysis Report

Detonate one sample in a sandbox, document its full behavioral chain, and combine it with your Week 6 static findings into one full report plus a derived detection rule.

Week 8: Memory Forensics & EDR Internals

  • Study why memory forensics matters: catching fileless malware and in-memory-only persistence
  • Practice acquiring a memory image from a lab VM and analyzing it with Volatility 3
  • Learn common Volatility plugins: pslist, pstree, netscan, malfind, and what each reveals
  • Study process injection detection in memory: hollowed processes, unusual memory regions
  • Learn how EDR agents work conceptually: kernel callbacks, ETW, behavioral telemetry collection
  • Study how EDR detections differ from SIEM detections (endpoint behavior vs. aggregated log correlation)
  • Practice reviewing a sample EDR alert/timeline and reconstructing the attack sequence from it
  • Document a memory forensics walkthrough for one infected lab VM image
Volatility 3 Memory Forensics EDR Telemetry Fileless Malware

Task heading at the end of week: Memory Forensics Walkthrough

Acquire and analyze a memory image from an infected lab VM using Volatility 3, documenting your plugin-by-plugin findings and final reconstructed attack story.

🚀 Milestone 2 Achievement

You can run hypothesis-driven hunts, perform full static and dynamic malware analysis, write YARA detection rules, and conduct memory forensics — the deep technical toolkit that defines Tier 3 SOC work.

Phase 3: Incident Command, Automation & Career Readiness

Days 61-90: Leading major incidents, SOAR/automation engineering, and the certification/interview push

🎯 Phase 3 Goals

Incident Command SOAR Engineering Purple Teaming Executive Reporting Interview Readiness

Week 9: Major Incident Response & Command

  • Study the incident commander role: who leads a major incident and how authority/communication flows
  • Learn the full DFIR lifecycle: preparation, identification, containment, eradication, recovery, lessons learned
  • Practice leading a tabletop exercise for a multi-stage incident (initial access through lateral movement to impact)
  • Study containment strategy decisions: isolate now vs. observe-and-gather-more-intel tradeoffs
  • Learn how to coordinate with legal, PR/comms, and executive stakeholders during a live incident
  • Practice writing a real-time incident status update for non-technical executives mid-incident
  • Study chain-of-custody and evidence handling requirements if law enforcement/legal involvement is possible
  • Write a full incident command playbook for a ransomware scenario from detection through recovery
Incident Command DFIR Lifecycle Containment Strategy Chain of Custody

Task heading at the end of week: Ransomware Incident Command Playbook

Write a complete incident command playbook for a ransomware scenario, covering roles, containment decisions, stakeholder communication, and recovery steps.

Week 10: SOAR Engineering & Automation

  • Study SOAR (Security Orchestration, Automation and Response) concepts and where Tier 3 analysts design playbooks rather than just running them
  • Practice building a SOAR-style automated enrichment playbook (e.g. auto-enrich an IP with threat intel on alert creation)
  • Learn how to script automated triage logic in Python using SIEM/EDR APIs
  • Study auto-containment guardrails: how to automate response actions safely without causing self-inflicted outages
  • Practice writing a Python script that queries a threat intel API and enriches a sample alert with context
  • Learn how playbook metrics (mean time to respond, % auto-resolved) demonstrate automation program value
  • Study how detection engineering (Phase 1) and SOAR automation connect into one continuous improvement loop
  • Document a full automated triage workflow diagram for one common alert type
SOAR Python Automation Auto-Enrichment Playbook Design

Task heading at the end of week: Automated Enrichment Script

Write a Python script that takes a sample alert, queries a threat intel API for enrichment, and outputs a triage-ready summary — document it as a reusable SOAR playbook design.

Week 11: Purple Teaming & Detection Validation

  • Study purple teaming as the collaborative loop between offensive testing and defensive detection improvement
  • Learn how to use an adversary emulation framework (Atomic Red Team) to safely test your own detection coverage
  • Practice running 5 Atomic Red Team tests against your lab SIEM and confirming whether your rules actually fire
  • Study breach-and-attack simulation (BAS) concepts as a continuous, automated version of purple teaming
  • Learn how to write a detection validation report: technique tested, expected detection, actual result, gap closed
  • Practice tuning a Sigma rule that failed to fire during testing until it correctly detects the simulated technique
  • Study how purple team findings feed back into both detection engineering and the ATT&CK heatmap from Week 1
  • Compile your 90-day labs into a single portfolio-style master detection validation report
Purple Teaming Atomic Red Team BAS Detection Validation

Task heading at the end of week: Detection Validation Report

Run 5 Atomic Red Team tests against your lab environment, document detection results for each, and tune any rule that failed to fire correctly.

Week 12: Certification & Interview Readiness

  • Review and attempt practice questions aligned to a target cert (e.g. GCFA, GCIH, GREM, or CySA+)
  • Do a full mock major incident response under time pressure: detect, investigate, contain, and brief in under 4 hours
  • Prepare answers for common Tier 3 interview questions (walk through a hunt, explain a malware analysis, lead a mock incident debrief)
  • Build a one-page resume bullet list translating your 90 days into measurable skills/achievements
  • Polish your GitHub: pin your best 3 projects (Sigma rule pack, malware analysis report, incident playbook) with clean READMEs
  • Practice a mock technical interview (explain a full attack chain end-to-end out loud, attacker and defender view both)
  • Research target SOCs/MSSPs/industries and tailor your resume + LinkedIn headline accordingly
  • Plan your next 90 days: pick a specialization (DFIR lead, malware reverse engineering, or detection engineering management)
Mock Interviews Resume Building GitHub Portfolio Cert Prep

Task heading at the end of week: Job-Ready Tier 3 Portfolio

Finalize a GitHub portfolio, resume bullets, and one polished master detection/incident report — your complete application package for Tier 3 SOC Analyst roles.

🏆 Final Achievement

Congratulations! You now have a working Tier 3 toolkit (detection engineering, threat intelligence, threat hunting, malware/memory forensics, incident command, SOAR automation, purple teaming) plus a portfolio of real reports and a job-ready application package — the foundation of a genuinely senior SOC Analyst.

  • Look into SOC management/team lead as a leadership path
  • Consider detection engineering as a dedicated, tooling-heavy specialization
  • Specialize in a high-stakes industry vertical (finance, critical infrastructure, healthcare)
  • Pursue an advanced cert aligned to your chosen lane

Stay curious, document everything, and always close the loop back into detection.
